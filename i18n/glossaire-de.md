# German translation guide and glossary (Leitfaden für die deutsche Übersetzung)

Essay: *Sortir de la religion sans en fonder une autre ?* by Thomas Gourouza (October 2026). German title: **Die Religion verlassen, ohne eine andere zu gründen?**

This guide binds every translator and reviewer. Write the translation in German. When in doubt, choose fidelity to the argument, then natural, precise German prose, never a calque.

## 1. Register and voice

- Scholarly German of a serious essay in the history of ideas (the register of a good Suhrkamp or Meiner translation of French philosophy), clear and readable, avoiding needless nominal chains. Current German orthography (neue Rechtschreibung). Keep the author's first person (ich, mein Kriterium) exactly where the French has it.
- Translate meaning, rhythm and argument. Long French sentences may be split, but never drop a clause, a qualification or a hedge (peut-être = vielleicht, sans doute = wohl). Never add explanations.
- **Never use the em dash (—) or the en dash (–), anywhere.** This is a strict rule of the site, also in German: where German would use a Gedankenstrich, rephrase or use a comma, a colon, a semicolon or parentheses. Date ranges use a plain hyphen (1804-1872).

## 2. Structure: keep it identical, it is machine-checked

- Same Markdown: same headings (#, ##, ###, ####), same number of paragraphs in the same order, same lists, tables (same rows and columns), block quotes, italics (*…*), bold (**…**). One French paragraph = one German paragraph.
- Heading formats: `## Erster Teil: die Alten und die Monotheismen vor der Vernunft` ; author sections keep the pattern `### Name (dates): formula`, e.g. `### Platon (428/427-348/347 v. Chr.): die erste Theologie und das erste Gefängnis für Gottlose`.
- Literary boxes. The first line keeps the **literal French machine marker** `> **En littérature.**` unchanged (the site displays it translated), then the works in the exact pattern `Author, *Title* (date)` separated by ` ; ` (space, semicolon, space); a title the French puts in « » goes in „ “. Paragraphs inside the box stay separated by a line containing only `>`. The last paragraph starts with `Quellen:` (or `Quelle:`).
- Keep every reference exactly: fragment numbers (fr. 233 = Fr. 233), paragraphs (§ 125), pages (p. 16 = S. 16), Bible references (Gn 1, 27 ; Rm 12, 1: keep this French biblical notation unchanged, it is machine-read), numerals, years.

## 3. Quotations (the most important rule)

The essay quotes texts in their original language, then gives a French rendering.
- The **original-language quotation is copied exactly**, character for character (Greek, Latin, English, Russian, Hebrew, Danish, Italian, Spanish, and German in its original spelling: Nietzsche's, Feuerbach's, Kant's or Stirner's historical orthography stays as quoted, e.g. "Erkenntniß", "Christenthums"). Never correct, modernize or retranslate it.
- The **French rendering that follows it** is translated into German, from the original-language text when you can read it.
- When the original is **already German** (Nietzsche, Hegel, Feuerbach, Stirner, Marx, Engels, Schleiermacher, Kant, Schopenhauer, Freud, Weber, Otto, Heidegger, Wittgenstein, Barth, Bonhoeffer, Schmitt, Blumenberg, Löwith, Habermas, Jaspers, Goethe, Hölderlin, Rilke, Kafka…), keep it exactly and **drop the French rendering**, adjusting the sentence so nothing dangles. This will happen very often: check each case.
- When the original is **French** (Pascal, Montaigne, Descartes, Rousseau, Voltaire, Bayle, Comte, Renan, Bergson, Durkheim, Weil, Sartre, Camus, Ricœur, Levinas, Gauchet…), keep the French quotation exactly and follow it with your German rendering: „French original“, German rendering.
- When the essay quotes a **published French translation by name** (Mongault, Albert, Hamelin, Munk, Saunders, Gauthier…), keep that French wording verbatim as a quotation of that translation, and make sure a German rendering of the original is present nearby. When it quotes a French translation of a German text, the German original makes it unnecessary: keep the reference, no gloss.
- Never cite or invent a published German translation by name: the German renderings are ours and unattributed.
- Typography: German „ “, nested ‚ ‘. Quotations stay inside the quotation marks.

## 9. Annexes and lexicon

Annexes are neutral reading notes written without any quotation: keep them quotation-free, third person, present tense, no verdict. The header fields `categorie`, `ordre`, `sections`, `oeuvres` are copied unchanged; `titre`, `motifs` (the German phrases that will be auto-linked in the German essay: two to six exact German expressions as the German essay uses them, with the inflected forms that actually occur when frequent; precise, never a bare common word) and the body are translated; `references` keep the bibliographic titles as given, translating only connecting words. The final paragraph beginning «Ce que l'essai suppose» becomes **Was der Essay voraussetzt**.
