# Spanish translation guide and glossary (guía para la traducción al español)

Essay: *Sortir de la religion sans en fonder une autre ?* by Thomas Gourouza (October 2026). Spanish title: **¿Salir de la religión sin fundar otra?**

This guide binds every translator and reviewer. Write the translation in Spanish. When in doubt, choose fidelity to the argument, then natural, precise Spanish prose, never a calque.

## 1. Register and voice

- Scholarly Spanish of a serious essay in the history of ideas (the register of a good Trotta, Tecnos or Akal translation of French philosophy), clear and readable, neutral between Spain and Latin America (no regional idioms; *vosotros* never needed). Keep the author's first person (yo, mi criterio) exactly where the French has it.
- Translate meaning, rhythm and argument. Long French sentences may be split, but never drop a clause, a qualification or a hedge (peut-être = quizá, tal vez ; sans doute = sin duda, probablemente). Never add explanations.
- **Never use the em dash (—) or the en dash (–), anywhere.** This is a strict rule of the site, also in Spanish, where dialogue and incisos often use the raya: rephrase, or use commas, a colon, a semicolon or parentheses. Date ranges use a plain hyphen (1804-1872).
- Spanish punctuation: opening ¿ and ¡ are required.

## 2. Structure: keep it identical, it is machine-checked

- Same Markdown: same headings (#, ##, ###, ####), same number of paragraphs in the same order, same lists, tables (same rows and columns), block quotes, italics (*…*), bold (**…**). One French paragraph = one Spanish paragraph.
- Heading formats: `## Primera parte: los Antiguos y los monoteísmos ante la razón` ; author sections keep the pattern `### Name (dates): formula`, e.g. `### Platón (428/427-348/347 a. C.): la primera teología, y la primera cárcel para los impíos`.
- Literary boxes. The first line keeps the **literal French machine marker** `> **En littérature.**` unchanged (the site displays it translated), then the works in the exact pattern `Author, *Title* (date)` separated by ` ; ` (space, semicolon, space); a title the French puts in « » stays in « ». Paragraphs inside the box stay separated by a line containing only `>`. The last paragraph starts with `Fuentes:` (or `Fuente:`).
- Keep every reference exactly: fragment numbers (fr. 233), paragraphs (§ 125), pages (p. 16), Bible references (Gn 1, 27 ; Rm 12, 1: keep this French biblical notation unchanged, it is machine-read), numerals, years.

## 3. Quotations (the most important rule)

The essay quotes texts in their original language, then gives a French rendering.
- The **original-language quotation is copied exactly**, character for character (Greek, Latin, German, English, Russian, Hebrew, Danish, Italian…). Never correct, modernize or retranslate it.
- The **French rendering that follows it** is translated into Spanish, from the original-language text when you can read it.
- When the original is **already Spanish** (Teresa de Ávila, Juan de la Cruz, Unamuno, Borges, Cervantes, Alvar…), keep it exactly and **drop the French rendering**, adjusting the sentence so nothing dangles.
- When the original is **French** (Pascal, Montaigne, Descartes, Rousseau, Voltaire, Bayle, Comte, Renan, Bergson, Durkheim, Weil, Sartre, Camus, Ricœur, Levinas, Gauchet…), keep the French quotation exactly and follow it with your Spanish rendering: «French original», Spanish rendering.
- When the essay quotes a **published French translation by name** (Mongault, Albert, Hamelin, Munk, Saunders, Gauthier…), keep that French wording verbatim as a quotation of that translation, and make sure a Spanish rendering of the original is present nearby.
- Never cite or invent a published Spanish translation by name: the Spanish renderings are ours and unattributed.
- Typography: Spanish angular quotes « », nested “ ”. Quotations stay inside the quotation marks.

## 9. Annexes and lexicon

Annexes are neutral reading notes written without any quotation: keep them quotation-free, third person, present tense, no verdict. The header fields `categorie`, `ordre`, `sections`, `oeuvres` are copied unchanged; `titre`, `motifs` (the Spanish phrases that will be auto-linked in the Spanish essay: two to six exact Spanish expressions as the Spanish essay uses them, with the inflected forms that actually occur when frequent; precise, never a bare common word) and the body are translated; `references` keep the bibliographic titles as given, translating only connecting words. The final paragraph beginning «Ce que l'essai suppose» becomes **Lo que el ensayo supone**. Lexicon entries (`i18n/es/lexique/part-*.json`, `{fr, terme, langue?, motifs, definition}`) follow the same rules, with definitions of 90 to 220 words.
