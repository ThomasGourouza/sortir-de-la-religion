# English translation guide and glossary

Essay: *Sortir de la religion sans en fonder une autre ?* by Thomas Gourouza (October 2026). English title: **Leaving Religion Without Founding Another?**

This guide binds every translator and reviewer. When in doubt, choose fidelity to the argument, then idiomatic English, never a calque.

## 1. Register and voice

- Scholarly but readable British-neutral English (use -ize spellings: secularization, civilization), the register of a serious essay in the history of ideas, not a textbook. Keep the author's first person (I, my criterion) exactly where the French has it.
- Translate meaning, rhythm and argument, not word order. Long French sentences may be split when English requires it, but never drop a clause, a qualification or a hedge (peut-être, sans doute, selon une formule rapportée…). Never add explanations.
- Never use the em dash or the en dash, anywhere, including date ranges: use commas, colons, semicolons, parentheses, and a plain hyphen in ranges (1804-1872).

## 2. Structure: keep it identical, it is machine-checked

- Same Markdown: same headings (#, ##, ###, ####), same number of paragraphs in the same order, same lists, tables (same rows and columns), block quotes, italics (*…*), bold (**…**). One French paragraph = one English paragraph.
- Heading formats: `## Part One: the Ancients and the monotheisms before reason` ; author sections keep the pattern `### Name (dates): formula`, e.g. `### Plato (428/427-348/347 BCE): the first theology, and the first prison for the impious`.
- Literary boxes. The first line keeps the **literal French machine marker** `> **En littérature.**` unchanged (the site displays it translated), then the works in the exact pattern `Author, *Title* (date)` separated by ` ; ` (space, semicolon, space), quoted titles in “ ”. Translate author names and titles per sections 6 and 7. Paragraphs inside the box stay separated by a line containing only `>`. The last paragraph starts with `Sources:` (or `Source:`).
- Keep every reference exactly: fragment numbers (fr. 233), paragraphs (§ 125), pages (p. 16), Bible references (Gn 1, 27 ; Rm 12, 1 keep this French biblical notation unchanged), numerals, years.

## 3. Quotations (the most important rule)

The essay quotes texts in their original language, then gives a French rendering.
- The **original-language quotation is copied exactly**, character for character (Greek, Latin, German, Russian, Hebrew, Arabic transliteration, Danish, Italian, Spanish, English…). Never correct, modernize or retranslate it.
- The **French rendering that follows it** is translated into English. Translate from the original-language text when you can read it, so the English is faithful to the source, not to the French.
- When the original is **already English** (James, Clifford, Hume, Murdoch, Russell…), keep it and **drop the French rendering**, adjusting the sentence so nothing dangles.
- When the original is **French** (Pascal, Montaigne, Descartes, Rousseau, Voltaire, Bayle, Comte, Renan, Bergson, Durkheim, Weil, Sartre, Camus, Ricœur, Levinas, Gauchet…), keep the French quotation exactly, in “ ”, and follow it with your English rendering, in the same way the essay renders other languages: “French original”, English rendering.
- When the essay quotes a **published French translation by name** (Mongault, Albert, Hamelin, Munk, Saunders, Gauthier…), keep that French wording verbatim as a quotation of that translation, and make sure an English rendering of the original is present nearby (translate the essay's own surrounding gloss).
- Never cite or invent a published English translation (no Kaufmann, no Hollingdale, no Garnett): the English renderings are ours and unattributed.
- Typography: English curly quotes “ ”, nested ‘ ’ ; French « » become “ ”. Quotations stay inside the quotation marks; English punctuation outside unless part of the quotation.

## 9. Annexes and lexicon

Annexes are neutral reading notes written without any quotation: keep them quotation-free, third person, present tense, no verdict. The header fields `categorie`, `ordre`, `sections`, `oeuvres` are copied unchanged; `titre`, `motifs` (the English phrases that will be auto-linked in the English essay: give the exact English expressions the English essay uses for the notion, two to six, precise, never a bare common word) and the body are translated; `references` keep the bibliographic titles as given, translating only connecting words. Keep the final paragraph heading «Ce que l'essai suppose» as **What the essay assumes**.
