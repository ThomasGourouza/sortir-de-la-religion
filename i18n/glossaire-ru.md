# Russian translation guide and glossary (руководство для перевода на русский)

Essay: *Sortir de la religion sans en fonder une autre ?* by Thomas Gourouza (October 2026). Russian title: **Выйти из религии, не основав другую?**

This guide binds every translator and reviewer. Write the translation in Russian. When in doubt, choose fidelity to the argument, then natural literary Russian, never a calque.

## 1. Register and voice

- Scholarly Russian of a serious essay in the history of ideas (the register of good Russian translations of French philosophy), clear and readable, not bureaucratic. Keep the author's first person (я, мой критерий) exactly where the French has it.
- Translate meaning, rhythm and argument. Long French sentences may be split, but never drop a clause, a qualification or a hedge (peut-être = возможно, может быть; sans doute = вероятно). Never add explanations.
- **Never use the em dash (—) or the en dash (–), anywhere.** This is a strict rule of the site, also in Russian: where Russian would put a dash (between subject and predicate, in apposition, in dialogue), rephrase, or use a colon, a comma, parentheses, «это», «есть». Date ranges use a plain hyphen (1804-1872).

## 2. Structure: keep it identical, it is machine-checked

- Same Markdown: same headings (#, ##, ###, ####), same number of paragraphs in the same order, same lists, tables (same rows and columns), block quotes, italics (*…*), bold (**…**). One French paragraph = one Russian paragraph.
- Heading formats: `## Часть первая: древние и монотеизмы перед лицом разума` ; author sections keep the pattern `### Name (dates): formula`, e.g. `### Платон (428/427-348/347 до н. э.): первая теология и первая тюрьма для нечестивых`.
- Literary boxes. The first line keeps the **literal French machine marker** `> **En littérature.**` unchanged (the site displays it translated), then the works in the exact pattern `Author, *Title* (date)` separated by ` ; ` (space, semicolon, space); a title that the French puts in « » stays in « ». Paragraphs inside the box stay separated by a line containing only `>`. The last paragraph starts with `Источники:` (or `Источник:`).
- Keep every reference exactly: fragment numbers (fr. 233 = фр. 233), paragraphs (§ 125), pages (p. 16 = с. 16), Bible references (Gn 1, 27 ; Rm 12, 1: keep this French biblical notation unchanged, it is machine-read), numerals, years.

## 3. Quotations (the most important rule)

The essay quotes texts in their original language, then gives a French rendering.
- The **original-language quotation is copied exactly**, character for character (Greek, Latin, German, English, Hebrew, Danish, Italian, Spanish…). Never correct, modernize or retranslate it.
- The **French rendering that follows it** is translated into Russian, from the original-language text when you can read it.
- When the original is **already Russian** (Dostoevsky, Tolstoy, Belinsky…), keep it exactly and **drop the French rendering**, adjusting the sentence so nothing dangles.
- When the original is **French** (Pascal, Montaigne, Descartes, Rousseau, Voltaire, Bayle, Comte, Renan, Bergson, Durkheim, Weil, Sartre, Camus, Ricœur, Levinas, Gauchet…), keep the French quotation exactly and follow it with your Russian rendering: «French original», Russian rendering.
- When the essay quotes a **published French translation by name** (Mongault, Albert, Hamelin, Munk, Saunders, Gauthier…), keep that French wording verbatim as a quotation of that translation, and make sure a Russian rendering of the original is present nearby. For Dostoevsky, the Russian original makes the French translation unnecessary: keep the reference to it but no gloss is needed.
- Never cite or invent a published Russian translation by name: the Russian renderings are ours and unattributed.
- Typography: Russian « », nested „ “. Quotations stay inside the quotation marks.

## 9. Annexes and lexicon

Annexes are neutral reading notes written without any quotation: keep them quotation-free, third person, present tense, no verdict. The header fields `categorie`, `ordre`, `sections`, `oeuvres` are copied unchanged; `titre`, `motifs` (the Russian phrases that will be auto-linked in the Russian essay: two to six exact Russian expressions as the Russian essay uses them, in the grammatical forms that actually occur, for instance nominative and genitive when both are frequent; precise, never a bare common word) and the body are translated; `references` keep the bibliographic titles as given, translating only connecting words. The final paragraph beginning «Ce que l'essai suppose» becomes **Что предполагает эссе**.
