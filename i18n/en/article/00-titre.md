# Leaving Religion Without Founding Another?

*A debate from Plato to the present: critiques of religion, sciences of the religious, and spiritualities without God*




> **Quotation convention.** Each quotation carries its status. : found word for word in the downloaded source text (files listed at the end of the essay; when that text comes from imperfect optical character recognition, I say so and I normalize the spelling). : reported by a named secondary source, the original text not having been consulted. The essay contains no quotation restored from memory: what could not be checked is paraphrased without quotation marks and flagged as such. Translations without a translator's name are my own.
>
> **Debate convention.** The authors of this essay answer one another, but not all in the same way. I speak of an **attested response** when an author actually read another and answered him, in a text that can be cited: Hegel against Schleiermacher, Stirner against Feuerbach, Marx against Stirner, Freud against Romain Rolland, Origen against Celsus, Spinoza against Maimonides, Leibniz against Bayle, Voltaire against Leibniz, Rousseau against Voltaire, Dostoevsky against Voltaire, Goguel against Couchoud, William James against Clifford, Leo Strauss against Spinoza, Blumenberg against Löwith, Heidegger against Leibniz, Marion against Heidegger. I speak of a **constructed confrontation** when it is I who bring together two lines of thought that history did not set face to face: Bayle facing Ivan Karamazov, Montaigne facing Hume. Both are legitimate; to confuse them would be an error.
