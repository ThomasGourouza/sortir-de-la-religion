Leo Strauss (1899-1973), a Jew from Hesse, a student of Cassirer who attended the lectures of Husserl and Heidegger, a researcher at the Academy for the Science of Judaism in Berlin, who emigrated in 1932 to Paris, London and then New York, and taught at the New School and above all at Chicago from 1949 to 1968, founded a school of reading classical texts and put the theologico-political problem, the quarrel between reason and revelation, back at the centre of political philosophy.

#### Spinoza, Maimonides and the theologico-political problem

*Spinoza's Critique of Religion* (1930) examines whether the modern critique of revelation, from Spinoza to the Enlightenment, has refuted orthodoxy, and concludes that it has not: reason cannot demonstrate the impossibility of a mysterious and omnipotent God, and Spinoza won by laughter and mockery, not by proof; the 1965 preface recounts the path of Strauss, a young Zionist, through this theologico-political predicament. *Philosophy and Law* (1935) discovers in Maimonides and the falasifa a forgotten position, philosophy under the revealed law, in which revelation founds the city and philosophy inquires freely within that framework. Hence the thesis set out in *Persecution and the Art of Writing* (1952): philosophers living under threat wrote on two levels, an exoteric teaching in keeping with opinion and an esoteric teaching reserved for attentive readers, and one must read between the lines of Maimonides, Halevi and Spinoza, but also of Plato, Xenophon and Machiavelli.

#### Natural right and history

*Natural Right and History* (1953) contrasts classical natural right, Plato, Aristotle, Aquinas, founded on a hierarchy of ends and knowledge of the best regime, with modern natural right, Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau, founded on the rights of the individual, and with historicism, which denies all natural right and leads to nihilism, Weber and Heidegger included. In a late essay, *The Three Waves of Modernity* (1975), Strauss describes modernity as built in three waves, Machiavelli, Rousseau, Nietzsche, each lowering the ends in order to ensure their realization. The quarrel of the Ancients and the Moderns must be reopened, and Strauss takes the side of the Ancients, not to return to them but to recover the questions they asked. Athens and Jerusalem, philosophy and revelation, are the two roots of the West, and neither has refuted the other: their tension is the secret of its vitality, and the philosopher must live within this open question.

#### The school and the controversies

At Chicago Strauss trained disciples, Bloom, Jaffa, Mansfield, Pangle, and the adjective Straussian designates both a method of reading and, since the 2000s, a supposed influence on American politics, which his students dispute. His 1932 review of Schmitt's *The Concept of the Political* and his correspondence with Löwith, with Kojève, around *On Tyranny*, and with Voegelin are major documents of the century's political thought.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with the 1930 thesis and its 1965 preface, philosophy under the law, the art of writing, which it judges fragile in its general use, classical and modern natural right, Athens and Jerusalem, and the review of Schmitt. It regards Strauss as the adversary of the very idea of leaving religion, neither a refounder nor an exception, whose objection is not lifted: the exit is not an accomplished event but a question that remains open, and the Spinozist exception rests on an indemonstrable decision.
