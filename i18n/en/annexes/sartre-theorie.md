Jean-Paul Sartre (1905-1980), a graduate of the École normale supérieure, a lycée teacher, a reader of Husserl in Berlin in 1933-1934, a prisoner in 1940, a playwright, a novelist, founder of *Les Temps modernes* in 1945, a fellow traveller of the Communist Party from 1952 to 1956, who refused the Nobel Prize in 1964 and kept company with the Maoists after 1968, was for thirty years the very figure of the committed philosopher and of French intellectual atheism.

#### Phenomenology and Being and Nothingness

The early essays, *The Transcendence of the Ego* (1936) and *The Imaginary* (1940), apply phenomenology to consciousness, which is nothing other than its relation to the world, and *Nausea* (1938) makes Roquentin feel the raw contingency of existence before the root of a chestnut tree. *Being and Nothingness* (1943), an essay in phenomenological ontology, distinguishes two regions of being: being-in-itself, the being of things, full, opaque, identical with itself; being-for-itself, consciousness, which is what it is not and is not what it is, a nothingness at the heart of being, and therefore freedom. Man is condemned to be free, and he flees this freedom in bad faith, which plays at being a thing, the café waiter, or at being nothing but freedom. The gaze of the other constitutes me as an object and grounds the conflict of consciousnesses, the hell-is-other-people of *No Exit*. The desire to be God, in-itself-for-itself, the foundation of oneself, is man's useless passion. An ethics that was announced was never written; the *Notebooks for an Ethics* are posthumous.

#### Existentialism and commitment

*Existentialism Is a Humanism* (1946), a lecture that became a manifesto, sums up: existence precedes essence, there is no human nature because there is no God to conceive it, man makes himself through his choices and commits all humanity, and one must draw all the consequences of a coherent atheist position, Dostoevsky having written that if God did not exist everything would be permitted. *What Is Literature?* (1948) defines the committed writer. The *Critique of Dialectical Reason* (1960) attempts to ground Marxism, the unsurpassable horizon of our time, in an anthropology of praxis, scarcity, the group in fusion and the practico-inert. The existential biographies, *Baudelaire*, *Saint Genet*, *The Family Idiot* on Flaubert, apply existential psychoanalysis.

#### The Words and religion

*The Words* (1964) tells of a childhood in which writing took the place of God: brought up in a family half Catholic and half Protestant, Sartre loses his faith around the age of twelve without drama and understands late that atheism is a cruel and long-term undertaking, and that his religion of literature was its heir. He argued with Gabriel Marcel and the Christian existentialists, broke with Merleau-Ponty, and his quarrel with Camus in 1952, in *Les Temps modernes*, concerned *The Rebel* and the violence of history. Heidegger answered him as early as 1947 that existentialism remained a humanist metaphysics.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with being-in-itself and being-for-itself, freedom, bad faith, the 1946 lecture, the formula attributed to Dostoevsky and turned into a task, *The Words* and Heidegger's attested response. It treats Sartre as a lucid witness for the prosecution, not as an exception, without a spiritual remainder, a man of the marketplace of § 125, whose religion of writing is avowed, and it sides with Camus on substance and with Sartre on a point of method in the quarrel of 1952.
