Michel Onfray (b. 1959), son of a Norman farm labourer, brought up in a Salesian orphanage, a doctor of philosophy, a teacher at a technical secondary school in Caen until 2002, founder of the Université populaire de Caen, an omnipresent media polemicist and the author of more than a hundred books, defended a materialist and libertarian hedonism before moving towards a defence of Judaeo-Christian civilization that surprised his readers.

#### Hedonism and the Contre-histoire

The first books, *Le Ventre des philosophes* (1989), *L'Art de jouir* (1991), *La Sculpture de soi* (1993), winner of the Prix Médicis for non-fiction, propose a hedonist morality, to enjoy and give enjoyment without harming, nourished by Epicurus, Lucretius, Nietzsche and the libertines, and an aesthetics of existence. *La Politique du rebelle* (1997) extends it into a Nietzschean anarchism. The *Contre-histoire de la philosophie*, in twelve volumes from 2006 to 2020, drawn from the lectures of the Université populaire de Caen, founded in 2002 against the rise of the Front national, rehabilitates the philosophers whom the idealist tradition is said to have obscured, Cyrenaics, Epicureans, Montaigne, Gassendi, libertines, d'Holbach, Helvétius, Bentham, Feuerbach, Nietzsche, Bataille, Camus, against Plato, Descartes, Kant and Hegel.

#### The Traité d'athéologie

The *Traité d'athéologie* (2005), which sold several hundred thousand copies and was translated throughout the world, is the manifesto of French New Atheism. Taking the word atheology from Bataille in the opposite sense, Onfray attacks the three monotheisms as death drives, hatred of reason, of the body, of women and of pleasure, and deconstructs the figure of Jesus, a conceptual character rather than a historical man; he denounces in the philosophers a Christian atheism, which keeps the values of Christianity while abandoning God, and calls for a post-Christian atheism. The book drew responses from René Rémond, Matthieu Baumier and Irène Fernandez, and a Spanish critique, by Jaime Alvar, which turns the charge of Christian atheism back against its author.

#### Décadence and Théorie de Jésus

After *Cosmos* (2015), a materialist ontology and a celebration of nature, *Décadence* (2017) tells of the birth, greatness and death of Judaeo-Christian civilization, from Jesus, who did not exist, to Islam, which would replace it: it is religion that produces a civilization, and ours is dying with its own. In 2020 Onfray founded the review *Front populaire*, and in 2021 he defended the Latin Mass against the pope as liturgical heritage, declaring that his world is the one made possible by the God of the Christians. *Théorie de Jésus* (2023) takes up the mythicist thesis: Jesus is a concept, manufactured by Paul and the evangelists from the prophecies, and his historical existence matters little. The trajectory, from libertarian hedonist to defender of Christian civilization, is the object of many criticisms, which point to errors of scholarship and to the contradiction between atheology and the defence of the Church.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with the *Traité d'athéologie*, Christian atheism, Bataille's atheology turned around, *Décadence*, the 2021 text in *Le Figaro* and *Théorie de Jésus*, which it connects with Strauss's gesture and Bauer's path. It sees in Onfray an inverted Marx, a contemporary Grand Inquisitor, for whom religion is false and necessary, a Christian atheist in a different way from Comte-Sponville, a Church without spirituality, and the contemporary case of Stirner's law: Onfray refounds a collective absolute, Civilization, and has taken the step from soft refoundation to strict refoundation.
