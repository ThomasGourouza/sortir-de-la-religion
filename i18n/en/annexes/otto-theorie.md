Rudolf Otto (1869-1937), Lutheran theologian, professor at Göttingen, Breslau and then Marburg, traveller in North Africa, India, China and Japan, liberal deputy in the Prussian parliament, wrote in 1917 one of the most widely read books of twentieth-century theology, *Das Heilige*, which gave the phenomenology of religion its central concept, the numinous.

#### The Idea of the Holy

The book starts from an observation: theology and the philosophy of religion have treated the divine through rational concepts, goodness, omnipotence, personality, and have missed what makes up the proper object of religion, an irrational element, in the sense of non-conceptual, which Otto calls the numinous, from the Latin numen, to distinguish it from the holy, already charged with moral meaning. The numinous is not defined, it is awakened: Otto invites the reader who has never felt a religious emotion not to read on. It is described through its moments. The Kreaturgefühl, creature-feeling, is the echo in the subject of a reality that surpasses it, and Otto thereby corrects Schleiermacher: dependence is not primary, it presupposes the object. The mysterium tremendum combines awe, majesty and energy; the mysterium is the wholly other, ganz Andere, which fits into no category; the fascinans attracts, enraptures and promises bliss. The numinous is an a priori category of the mind, as in Kant and in Jakob Fries, whose philosophy of religion Otto follows; it is schematized in history through rational concepts, and Christianity, in which the numinous is fully moralized without being lost, is for Otto the most accomplished religion. The book follows the manifestation of the numinous in the Old Testament, the Gospels, Luther, the mystics, art and music.

#### Comparative mysticism and India

In Marburg Otto founds a collection of world religions and a Religious League of Mankind. *Mysticism East and West* (1926) compares Eckhart and Shankara, the master of non-dualist Vedanta, to show common structures and fundamental differences; *India's Religion of Grace and Christianity* (1930) brings Vaishnava bhakti close to Lutheranism. These works make him, with Söderblom and Heiler, a founder of the comparative history of religions on a phenomenological basis, which Van der Leeuw and Eliade will pursue.

#### Legacy

The numinous has passed into everyday vocabulary; Jung takes it up to define religion, Eliade for the sacred, Barth rejects it as a human experience, Levinas combats it as violence. Critics have pointed to the circularity of the method, which addresses only those who have already had the experience, and to the ranking of Christianity as the summit.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with the numinous and its moments, the warning to the reader, the correction of Schleiermacher and the critique of James, which are attested responses, and the a priori category, from which it draws the conclusion that one cannot get out of the numinous but only let it lie fallow or cultivate it. It sees in Otto the paradoxical source of spirituality without a Church, places the oceanic feeling as a pure fascinans, and reports Levinas's response.
