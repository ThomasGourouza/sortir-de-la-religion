Sigmund Freud (1856-1939), a Viennese neurologist of Jewish origin, a student of Charcot, inventor of psychoanalysis in the 1890s, head of an international movement from 1910, who was driven out by the Anschluss in 1938 and died in London, transformed the conception of the mind and gave the critique of religion the regime of illusion.

#### The discovery of the unconscious

The *Studies on Hysteria* (1895), with Breuer, then *The Interpretation of Dreams* (1900) establish the method and the theory: symptoms, dreams, slips of the tongue and bungled actions have a meaning, and this meaning points to unconscious wishes, repressed because they cannot be reconciled with moral conscience. Repression, the return of the repressed, transference and free association are the concepts of the cure. The *Three Essays on the Theory of Sexuality* (1905) describe an infantile sexuality; the Oedipus complex, sketched as early as 1900 and named in 1910, desire for the parent of the other sex and rivalry with the parent of the same sex, is resolved by the formation of the superego. The second topography (1923) distinguishes the id, reservoir of the drives, the ego and the superego; *Beyond the Pleasure Principle* (1920) introduces the death drive alongside the life drives.

#### Religion

Freud wrote on religion in every period of his life. Obsessive Actions and Religious Practices (1907) compares rites to the ceremonials of neurotics. *Totem and Taboo* (1913) grounds religion, morality and society in an original murder: the sons of the primal horde killed and ate the father who kept the women, then, seized by remorse, deified him as a totem and forbade themselves incest; the totemic meal is the first sacrifice, and Christianity, in which the Son atones, is its avowal. *The Future of an Illusion* (1927) explains belief by infantile helplessness and the need for a father's protection, defines illusion by the wish that commands it, makes religion the universal obsessional neurosis, and wagers on education to reality and on reason, our god Logos. *Civilization and Its Discontents* (1930) answers Romain Rolland on the oceanic feeling, which it traces back to primary narcissism, and describes culture as a renunciation of drives that is a source of guilt. *Moses and Monotheism* (1939) makes Moses an Egyptian killed by his people, whose monotheism would be the return of the repressed.

#### Dissent and readings

Jung breaks away in 1913 over libido and religion, Adler before him, Rank and Reich afterwards; Pfister, a pastor, answers Freud with *The Illusion of a Future*. Lacan, Ricoeur, who counts him among the masters of suspicion, Marcuse, Girard, who turns *Totem and Taboo* around, and the critics of the scientific status of psychoanalysis, Popper, Grünbaum, have carried the debate further.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay takes as known the unconscious, repression, the Oedipus complex, *Totem and Taboo*, the definition of illusion, the universal obsessional neurosis, the god Logos and Rolland's letter. It holds Freud to be the representative of the regime of illusion, turns § 344 of *The Gay Science* against him and judges that he refounds a god, Logos, while granting both Freud and Rolland a share of the truth: religion without a father, but not without a mother.
