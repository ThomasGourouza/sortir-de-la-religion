Marx had regarded the critique of religion as completed and had turned to the critique of the economy; his heirs had to decide what the workers' movement, and then the socialist States, would do with religion. From combative materialism to State atheism, and from dialogue with Christianity to liberation theology, Marxism has been at once the secular religion par excellence and an interlocutor of Christianity.

#### Social democracy and Lenin

Engels, in his studies of early Christianity, saw in the first Christians a movement of the oppressed comparable to the workers' movement; Karl Kautsky, theorist of the Second International, makes of it in 1908 a materialist history, *Foundations of Christianity*, a communism of consumption among slaves and the poor. The Erfurt programme (1891) declares religion a private matter, a position Lenin combats from 1905: religion is a private matter with regard to the State, not with regard to the party, which must carry on atheist propaganda without making it a precondition of membership; militant materialism, he says in 1922, must ally itself with the bourgeois atheists of the eighteenth century. After 1917, atheism becomes State doctrine, the Orthodox Church is persecuted, the League of the Godless organizes propaganda, and Marxism-Leninism, with its classics, its dogmas, its trials and its personality cult, takes on, in the eyes of Berdyaev, Voegelin and Aron, the form of an inverted religion.

#### The heterodox

Antonio Gramsci, in his *Prison Notebooks*, analyses the Church as an apparatus of hegemony and religious common sense as a terrain of struggle, and compares Marxism to an intellectual and moral Reformation; Walter Benjamin blends Jewish messianism and historical materialism in his theses *On the Concept of History* (1940); Ernst Bloch, in *The Principle of Hope* (1954-1959) and *Atheism in Christianity* (1968), reads the Bible as a book of revolt, from Exodus to the Apocalypse, and holds that only an atheist can be a good Christian and only a Christian a good atheist; Lucien Goldmann links Pascal and Marxism through the wager; Roger Garaudy, for the French Communist Party, opens in the 1960s a dialogue with Christians, welcomed by the Second Vatican Council and the journal *Esprit*, before converting to Islam.

#### Liberation theology

In Latin America, after Medellín (1968), Gustavo Gutiérrez, Leonardo Boff, Jon Sobrino and Juan Luis Segundo construct a liberation theology that starts from the praxis of the poor, borrows from Marxism the analysis of structures of dependency and reads the Gospel as a historical liberation; the base communities and Archbishop Romero, assassinated in 1980, are its figures. Rome, through the 1984 instruction signed by Ratzinger, condemns its Marxist borrowings, and Michael Löwy has spoken of this current as a liberation Christianity. In Europe, the political theology of Metz and Moltmann and left-wing Christians are its parallels.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with Engels's texts, Lenin's position, State atheism, the reading of Marxism as a secular religion by Aron and Voegelin, and State Marxism among the strict refoundations. It retains the view that Marx dismissed the sacred without dismissing salvation, and that the Protestation he named remains the question the wisdoms have not settled; the article extends the essay towards a Christian Marxism and a liberation theology that it does not treat.
