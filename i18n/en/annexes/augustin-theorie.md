Aurelius Augustinus (354-430), born at Thagaste in Numidia, a rhetorician at Carthage, Rome and Milan, converted in 386, baptized by Ambrose, priest and then bishop of Hippo from 395 until his death, during the siege of the city by the Vandals, left a body of work of more than a hundred titles which shaped Latin theology, and through it Western thought, for more than a thousand years.

#### The itinerary and the Confessions

The *Confessions* (397-401), in thirteen books, recount to God, in the second person, Augustine's life up to his conversion: childhood, the theft of the pears, his studies, the reading of Cicero's *Hortensius*, which fired him with a love of wisdom, nine years among the Manichaeans, who set two principles against each other, good and evil, the scepticism of the New Academy, the discovery of the books of the Platonists, which led him to conceive an immaterial God and evil as privation, and finally the scene in the garden at Milan and the child's voice, take up and read. The last books meditate on memory, on time, which Augustine makes a distension of the soul, and on creation. The book is the first account of an inner life, and its formula, God more inward than my inmost self, founds Western interiority.

#### The City of God and the controversies

The *De civitate Dei* (413-426), written after the sack of Rome by Alaric in 410, answers the pagans who blamed Christianity for it: the first ten books refute Roman religion and philosophy, discussing above all the Platonists, the closest to the Christians; the twelve that follow tell the history of the two cities, the city of God and the earthly city, founded on two loves, the love of God to the point of contempt of self and the love of self to the point of contempt of God, intermingled until the judgement. Three controversies also structure the work. Against Manichaeism, Augustine defends the goodness of creation and free will. Against the Donatists, a schismatic Church of Africa that demanded the purity of its ministers, he holds that the validity of the sacraments does not depend on the holiness of the one who administers them, and ends by accepting imperial coercion to bring the schismatics back, invoking the Gospel's compel them to come in. Against Pelagius, a monk from Britain for whom man can avoid sin by his own strength, he elaborates the doctrine of grace and original sin: since Adam, humanity is a mass of perdition, no one can will the good without grace, and God predestines those whom he saves; Pelagianism is condemned in 418 and 431.

#### Knowledge and the Trinity

Against the sceptics, Augustine holds that whoever doubts exists, si fallor sum; seeks truth within the soul, where the inner teacher illuminates it; defines the method of believing in order to understand; and, in the *De Trinitate*, finds in memory, understanding and will the image of the Trinity. His theology of history, his doctrine of grace and his psychology nourished scholasticism, Luther, an Augustinian monk, Calvin, Jansenism and Pascal, Descartes and Heidegger.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay takes as known the narrative of the *Confessions*, in particular the reading of the Platonists, the plan of the *City of God* and its discussion of the Platonists in book VIII, the formula of interiority, the method of believing in order to understand, and the Donatist episode, which it cites without having reread it. It makes the Augustinian synthesis of the God of the philosophers and the God of the Bible the major fact of the intellectual history of the West, which Pascal and Heidegger undo, and sees in rational theology armed by the Christian emperors, for which Augustine supplied the justification against the Donatists, a strict refoundation.
