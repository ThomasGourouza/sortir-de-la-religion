Hans Blumenberg (1920-1996), born in Lübeck to a Jewish mother, excluded from the universities under Nazism, interned in 1945, professor at Hamburg, Giessen, Bochum and Münster, withdrawn from academic life in his last decades, left an immense and winding body of work, at once history of ideas, phenomenology and anthropology, of which the 1966 book on modernity is the most discussed.

#### Metaphorology

The *Paradigms for a Metaphorology* (1960) argue that certain metaphors, naked truth, light, the book of the world, shipwreck with spectator, are not ornaments translatable into concepts but absolute metaphors, which answer questions that the concept cannot handle, and whose history sheds light on the history of thought. *Die Lesbarkeit der Welt* (1981), *Shipwreck with Spectator* (1979) and *Höhlenausgänge* (1989) are applied metaphorologies, and the theory of nonconceptuality is their culmination.

#### The legitimacy of the modern age

*Die Legitimität der Neuzeit* (1966) attacks the secularization theorem, in Löwith, Schmitt and the theologians, according to which modern ideas, progress, autonomy, the State, are secularized Christian contents, hence illegitimate, unpaid debts to their origin. Blumenberg shows that this thesis presupposes an identity of substance between the Christian idea and the modern idea, which it cannot establish; what is identical is the function, the place in a system of questions, and modernity has reoccupied, Umbesetzung, positions left empty by theology by putting new answers in them, often required to answer inordinate questions, such as that of the meaning of history as a whole, which it would not have asked of itself. The legitimacy of modernity comes from its self-assertion, Selbstbehauptung, a response to the theological absolutism of late medieval nominalism, which had made God an arbitrary will and withdrawn every guarantee from the world: man had to take charge of himself through science and technology. Modernity is also the second overcoming of Gnosticism, after Augustine's, which had left the problem of evil in the creature. Schmitt replies in *Political Theology II* (1970), and the second edition in turn answers Löwith, Schmitt and Gadamer.

#### The Copernican world and myth

*The Genesis of the Copernican World* (1975) follows the reception of Copernicus as a history of the self-consciousness of decentred man. *Arbeit am Mythos*, *Work on Myth* (1979), argues that myth is not a stage surpassed by logos but a lasting response to the absolutism of reality, the anxiety before an unmastered world, which it keeps at a distance by naming and narrating; the myth of Prometheus, from Hesiod to Goethe and Kafka, is its thread. The posthumous works, among them the *Beschreibung des Menschen* (2006), develop a phenomenological anthropology.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay takes as known the secularization theorem and its critique, reoccupation, self-assertion, theological absolutism, the second overcoming of Gnosticism and the attested responses to Löwith and Schmitt. It concedes to Blumenberg that the extensive version of its thesis proves too much, makes the sacrifice criterion its answer, and regards Blumenberg as an unexpected ally, in the order of history.
