Celsus is a pagan philosopher of the second half of the 2nd century, author c. 178 of a treatise against the Christians, the *True Doctrine*, *Alèthès logos*, entirely lost but three-quarters reconstructed thanks to the quotations Origen makes from it in his refutation, seventy years later. It is the oldest comprehensive critique of Christianity, and almost all its arguments have had a long afterlife.

#### The man and the book

Nothing is known of Celsus apart from his book. Origen takes him for an Epicurean, but the text shows a Middle Platonist, steeped in Plato's *Timaeus* and *Letters*, believing in a supreme, transcendent God, who governs the world through subordinate gods and daemons, to whom the cities owe their worship. He knows Judaism, the Gospels, which he has read, and the divisions among Christians, including the Gnostics. The *True Doctrine* is built in two stages: Celsus first gives the floor to a Jew who attacks Jesus and the Christians from the point of view of the Law; then he speaks in his own name, from the point of view of philosophy and the Empire.

#### The arguments

Jesus, according to Celsus's Jew, was born of an adulteress and a soldier named Panthera, learned magic in Egypt, gathered disciples of low condition, was abandoned and executed as a criminal; his resurrection rests on the testimony of a hysterical woman. The Christians, according to Celsus, ask people to believe without examining and recruit among the ignorant, slaves, women and children, whom they forbid to listen to the wise; what is good in them, the idea of a single and good God, the immortality of the soul, judgement, they stole from Plato and the Greeks, distorting it; what is their own, the incarnation of a God in a mortal body, the resurrection of the flesh, the idea that God takes a particular interest in human beings, is absurd and unworthy of God; finally, they refuse the gods of the city, the service of the emperor and of the army, and thereby undermine the order of an Empire that tolerates them. Celsus invites them to join the common worship and to help the emperor, for if everyone did as they do, the Empire would fall into the hands of the barbarians. He never denies that Jesus existed.

#### Afterlife

The book mattered enough for Origen to agree, c. 248, to refute it book by book. Porphyry, a century after Celsus, and the emperor Julian would take up its arguments; the libertines, Voltaire, Renan and Nietzsche would rediscover them without always knowing it, and the *True Doctrine*, translated in 1925 by Louis Rougier, has become a classic of anti-Christian critique. Modern historians read it above all as the testimony of a religious conservative anxious for the unity of the Empire.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay takes as known the date and the reconstruction of the *True Doctrine*, the four families of accusations, Jesus as magician, faith without reason, an audience of the ignorant, plagiarism of Plato, and the fact that Celsus does not doubt the existence of Jesus. It makes him a religious conservative, ancestor of Voltaire, Maurras and the late Onfray, who defends the gods of the city against the religion of slaves, and judges that neither of the two adversaries, Celsus or Origen, leaves religion.
