Blaise Pascal (1623-1662), a precocious mathematician and physicist, inventor of the calculating machine, theorist of the vacuum and of probability, turns to religion after a first conversion in 1646 and the night of 23 November 1654, the account of which, the *Memorial*, he keeps on his person. Linked to Port-Royal, he defends the Jansenists in the *Provincial Letters* and dies at thirty-nine, leaving the fragments of an apology for Christianity, the *Pensées*.

#### Jansenism and the Provincial Letters

Jansenism takes its name from the *Augustinus* (1640) of Cornelius Jansen, bishop of Ypres, which takes up the Augustinian doctrine of grace in its strictest form: human nature is corrupt, efficacious grace is given to whom God wills, and man can do nothing without it. The abbey of Port-Royal, led by Mother Angélique Arnauld, and the solitaries attached to it, Antoine Arnauld, Nicole, Sacy, are its centre in France; the Jesuits, supporters of a sufficient grace offered to all and of an accommodating morality, its adversaries. Five propositions drawn from the *Augustinus* are condemned by Rome in 1653. When Arnauld is threatened with exclusion from the Sorbonne, Pascal publishes in 1656-1657, under a pseudonym, eighteen *Provincial Letters*, a masterpiece of irony that ridicules the casuistry of the Jesuits, the direction of intention, the sufficient grace that does not suffice, and turns public opinion. Placed on the Index, they do not prevent the persecution of Port-Royal, which will be destroyed in 1710.

#### The Pensées

The fragments found at Pascal's death, some of them sorted by him into bundles, were published from 1670 and re-edited in different orders, by Brunschvicg in 1897 by theme, by Lafuma and Sellier according to the copies. The apology was to start from the wretchedness of man without God, the disproportion between the two infinities, the impotence of reason, the diversion that turns us away from thinking about our condition, then show his greatness, the thinking reed, and explain this contradiction by the Fall, which only the Christian religion makes intelligible. Proofs of God from nature are set aside: God is hidden, and gives himself through the heart, which has its reasons that reason does not know, through the prophecies, the miracles and the figure of Christ. The fragment on the wager addresses whoever can neither believe nor decide: the infinite stake makes the choice of God reasonable, and practice, taking holy water, will give rise to belief. The doctrine of the three orders, of bodies, of minds, of charity, separated by an infinite distance, sums up the Pascalian hierarchy. In the *Pensées* the spirit of finesse is distinguished from the spirit of geometry, and Montaigne is at once their model and their adversary.

#### Legacy

Voltaire attacks the *Pensées* in 1734 as the work of a sublime misanthrope; the Romantics, Chateaubriand, Sainte-Beuve, author of *Port-Royal*, rehabilitate them; Nietzsche sees in Pascal the most instructive victim of Christianity; Kierkegaard, Unamuno, Shestov, Simone Weil, then the existentialists make him the father of a philosophy of existence set against the system.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with the *Memorial*, the Jansenist controversy, the hidden God, the wager in its practical reading, the heart and the opposition between the God of Abraham and the God of the philosophers, which it reads as a critique of religion in the name of faith, foreshadowing Heidegger and Marion. It places Pascal's attested response to Montaigne, the fragment on Descartes's flick of the finger, and the line of the exit from above, Kierkegaard, Barth, Weil.
