Abû l-Walîd Muhammad ibn Rushd (1126-1198), Averroes to the Latins, born in Cordoba into a family of jurists, was qadi of Seville and of Cordoba, physician to the Almohad caliph and commentator on Aristotle at the prince's request. Disgraced in 1195 and exiled for a time, he dies in Marrakesh. His work has had two destinies: almost forgotten in Islam, it shaped Latin philosophy.

#### The Commentator

Averroes commented on almost the whole of Aristotle, in three formats: the epitomes, the middle commentaries and the long commentaries, which follow the text sentence by sentence. Dante calls him the one who made the great commentary, and scholasticism names him simply the Commentator. His project is to restore Aristotle freed from the Neoplatonic additions of Avicenna: he rejects emanation and the Avicennian distinction between essence and existence, and defends the eternity of the world, natural causality and God's knowledge of himself as the principle of all things. In the *Long Commentary on the De anima*, he holds that the material intellect, the receptacle of intelligibles, is one and eternal for the whole of humanity, like the agent intellect: human beings think by joining it, and personal immortality cannot be demonstrated by reason. This is the thesis of the unity of the intellect, or monopsychism, which Thomas Aquinas will combat.

#### The defence of philosophy

Against al-Ghazali, who in *The Incoherence of the Philosophers* had condemned twenty theses of the falsafa, Averroes writes *The Incoherence of the Incoherence*, a point-by-point refutation that defends causality against occasionalism and the necessity of the laws of nature. *The Decisive Treatise*, a short juridical treatise, establishes that the revealed Law commands philosophical study for those capable of it, that truth does not contradict truth, that the passages of the Qur'an whose apparent meaning clashes with demonstration must be interpreted, and that this interpretation must not be divulged to the common people, whom it would disturb: human beings fall into demonstrative, dialectical and rhetorical classes, and each must receive the truth in the form that suits it. The *Exposition of the Methods of Proof* criticizes the theologians of the kalam. Averroes also commented on Plato's *Republic*, since he did not have the *Politics*.

#### Latin Averroism

Translated into Latin from the 1220s, in Toledo and then in Sicily, Averroes enters the University of Paris with Aristotle. Masters of arts, Siger of Brabant and Boethius of Dacia, teach the eternity of the world and the unity of the intellect as necessary conclusions of reason while professing the faith; Thomas Aquinas writes *On the Unity of the Intellect* (1270) against them, and in 1277 Bishop Étienne Tempier condemns two hundred and nineteen propositions, accusing these masters of holding that a thing can be true in philosophy and false in theology, the double truth, which none of them formulated in those terms. Averroism continues in Padua until the sixteenth century, with Pomponazzi, and in 1852 Renan devotes his thesis to it, *Averroès et l'averroïsme*, which makes it an ancestor of free inquiry. The *Guide for the Perplexed* of Maimonides, his contemporary and compatriot, answers neighbouring questions.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with the role of the Commentator, the quarrel with al-Ghazali, an attested response, the doctrine of the three classes of people and of reserved interpretation, and the legend of the double truth born of the condemnations of 1277. It concludes that Averroes neither leaves nor founds anything, but divides, and finds this solution again in Spinoza, Comte, Renan and Leo Strauss; the essay constructs a confrontation with Spinoza, who did not read *The Decisive Treatise* but targets the same arrangement when he attacks Maimonides in the *Theological-Political Treatise*, and Voegelin grants Averroes a point.
