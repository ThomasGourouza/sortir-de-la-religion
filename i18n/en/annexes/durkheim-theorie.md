Émile Durkheim (1858-1917), the son of a rabbi from Épinal, a graduate of the École normale supérieure who passed the agrégation in philosophy, professor at Bordeaux and then at the Sorbonne, founder of *L'Année sociologique* (1898) and of the French school of sociology, sought to make sociology an autonomous science, with its own object and method, and to give the Third Republic a secular morality. His last book is devoted to religion.

#### The method and the first books

*The Rules of Sociological Method* (1895) defines the social fact: ways of acting, thinking and feeling that are external to the individual and endowed with a power of coercion; they must be treated as things, and the social must be explained by the social, not by psychology. *The Division of Labour in Society* (1893) distinguishes the mechanical solidarity of traditional societies, founded on likeness and a strong collective consciousness, from the organic solidarity of modern societies, founded on interdependence; the passage from one to the other produces pathological forms, among them anomie, the absence of rules. *Suicide* (1897), the first great statistical study, shows that the suicide rate varies with the integration and regulation of groups, and distinguishes egoistic, altruistic, anomic and fatalistic suicide: Protestants kill themselves more than Catholics, the unmarried more than the married. Durkheim took part in the Dreyfus affair, theorized secular morality and education, and conceived collective representations as a reality in their own right.

#### The Elementary Forms

*The Elementary Forms of Religious Life* (1912) studies the totemism of the Australian Aborigines, taken to be the simplest religion, in order to reach religion in general. Religion is defined there as a system of beliefs and practices relating to sacred things, set apart and forbidden, which unite into one Church all those who adhere to them; the sacred, opposed to the profane, is the fundamental category. The totem is the emblem of the clan, and the force it represents is society itself, experienced in the effervescence of gatherings and projected onto a symbol; rites, negative and positive, periodically remake the group. The categories of thought, time, space, genus, cause, have a social and religious origin. There are therefore no false religions, and religion will not die, since society will always need to think and celebrate itself; new ideals will be born from hours of creative effervescence. The French Revolution, which made the Fatherland and Reason sacred, is the example.

#### Legacy

The Durkheimian school, Mauss, Hubert, Halbwachs, Granet, dominated the French social sciences; Lévi-Strauss, Bourdieu, Parsons, Goffman, Mary Douglas and Hervieu-Léger are its heirs. Criticism has targeted the choice of totemism, the reduction of religion to the social, contested by Bergson, Malinowski and Evans-Pritchard, and the supposed conservatism of a sociology of order.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with the definition of religion by the sacred and the Church, the thesis of the god as hypostasized society, effervescence, the critique of Comte's artificial cults and the pages on the Revolution. It regards Durkheim as one of the two strongest objections to its thesis, one does not leave the sacred, one changes it, and reports his attested dialogues with James and Bergson.
