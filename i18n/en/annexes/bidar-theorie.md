Abdennour Bidar (b. 1971), a graduate of the École normale supérieure, agrégé and doctor in philosophy, raised by a French mother who had converted to Islam and initiated into Sufism, a secondary-school teacher, then an adviser at the French Ministry of Education and a member of the Observatoire de la laïcité, has proposed a reform of Islam through the individual and a philosophy of the exit from religion that keeps from the religious the idea of a human superpower. The essay knows him at second hand, through Tauil and Demorgon.

#### Self islam

*Self islam* (2006), an autobiographical narrative and a manifesto, defends a personal Islam: each Muslim must reappropriate his faith in conscience, choose what he keeps of the tradition and become the author of his relation to God, against communal Islam and the authority of the doctors of the law. *L'Islam sans soumission* (2008) gives this programme its philosophical foundation, a Muslim existentialism: rereading Iqbal, the Indo-Pakistani thinker of *The Reconstruction of Religious Thought in Islam*, Bidar argues that the Quran calls man to become khalîf, God's lieutenant on earth, heir to his attributes, creative and free, and that submission, the meaning of the word islam, must be understood as the fulfilment of this vocation and not as obedience; God withdraws so that man may take over, and faith becomes confidence in human superpower.

#### Comment sortir de la religion

*Comment sortir de la religion* (2012) widens the question to the West and enters into dialogue with Gauchet: the exit from religion is not complete, because modern man has kept the need for the infinite without having a place for it, and because renouncing the gods remains a psychically unbearable sacrifice. What is needed, then, is an exit from above, which inherits from religion its aim at the infinite by transferring it to man, and a secular spirituality that cultivates this capacity for self-surpassing; the *Histoire de l'humanisme en Occident* (2014) looks to the Renaissance, the Enlightenment and Romanticism for the milestones of such a divinization of the human. The *Lettre ouverte au monde musulman*, written in October 2014, widely reread after the attacks of January 2015, published as a book the same year and translated into several languages, asks Muslims to face the sickness of their civilization instead of blaming it on the West, and the *Plaidoyer pour la fraternité* (2015) proposes a spiritual humanism common to believers and unbelievers. Bidar runs meditation and fraternity workshops and takes part in the debate on laïcité.

#### Reception

Hailed in the French media as the voice of a liberal Islam, Bidar has been criticized by Islamicists for an unhistorical reading of the Quran, by Muslims for a reform they consider corrosive, and by philosophers for the ambiguity of a human superpower inherited from God.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay takes as known self islam, man as khalîf, superpower, the dialogue with Gauchet and the sentence on the psychically unbearable sacrifice, while stating that it has read none of Bidar's books. It concludes, cautiously and by way of a constructed confrontation, that Bidar falls under the criterion, on the side of Feuerbach and Kirillov, and that the exit from religion is first of all Christian but not only Christian.
