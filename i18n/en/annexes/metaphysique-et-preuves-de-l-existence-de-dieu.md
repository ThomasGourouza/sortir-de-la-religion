Since Aristotle, metaphysics has been the science of being as being and of the supreme being; the proofs of the existence of God are its most debated component, from scholasticism to Kant. The essay meets them in Descartes, sees them dismantled by Kant, and reads in their very structure, with Heidegger, what he calls onto-theology.

#### The vocabulary of metaphysics

The title *Metaphysics* designates the books of Aristotle placed after the *Physics*; there the discipline is called first philosophy, or theological philosophy. Its concepts form the vocabulary of the whole tradition: being and beings, that which is; substance, what exists in itself, and accidents, what exists in something else; essence, what a thing is, and existence, the fact that it is; act and potency; the four causes; the necessary and the contingent. Scholasticism divides metaphysics into ontology, the science of being in general, and special metaphysics, which treats the soul, the world and God; Christian Wolff fixes this division in the eighteenth century, and it is this division that Kant criticizes. Causa sui, cause of itself, is the name that Spinoza, after Descartes's *Replies to Objections*, gives to the being that exists by the necessity of its own essence.

#### The three proofs

The tradition produced several arguments, which Kant reduces to three. The ontological argument, formulated by Anselm in the *Proslogion*: God is that than which nothing greater can be thought; but what exists in reality is greater than what exists in the understanding alone; therefore God exists. The monk Gaunilo objects to it with the perfect island, Thomas Aquinas rejects it, Descartes takes it up again in the fifth Meditation, existence belonging to the essence of the perfect being as the sum of its angles belongs to the triangle, and Leibniz demands that the possibility of God be proved first. The cosmological proof starts from the world: every motion, every cause, every contingent being points back to a first mover, a first cause, a necessary being, failing which one would regress to infinity; these are the first three of Aquinas's five ways, inherited from Aristotle and Avicenna, and the principle of sufficient reason gives its Leibnizian version. The physico-theological proof, or argument from design, infers an intelligent author from the order and purposiveness of the world; it is the most popular in the eighteenth century, the one Hume discusses in the *Dialogues* and that Darwin will shake.

#### Kant's critique and its aftermath

Kant, in the Transcendental Dialectic of the *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781), argues that existence is not a real predicate, a hundred real thalers containing no more than a hundred possible thalers, which ruins the ontological argument; that the cosmological proof presupposes it in passing from the necessary being to the perfect being; and that the physico-theological proof proves at best an architect of the world, not its creator. God becomes a regulative idea of reason, then a postulate of practical reason. After Kant, Hegel rehabilitates the proofs as elevations of the spirit, neo-Thomism defends them, Heidegger sees in their structure, the supreme being grounding beings in general, the essence of metaphysics as onto-theology, and analytic philosophy takes them up again in modal and probabilistic forms, in Plantinga and Swinburne.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with the vocabulary of being and beings, the difference between the three proofs, the place of Descartes, who takes from theology the task of proving God and entrusts it to philosophy, Kant's critique, which dismantles that component, and Heidegger's reading, which makes the causa sui a god before whom one can neither pray nor dance. It holds the fusion of the God of the proofs and the God of the Bible to be the major fact that Pascal and Heidegger each undo in their own way.
