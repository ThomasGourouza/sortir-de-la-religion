Bruno Bauer (1809-1882), a Hegelian theologian in Berlin, first placed on the right of the school, moves in a few years to the most radical critique of the Gospels and of the Christian state, loses his chair in Bonn in 1842, leads the Berlin circle of the Free Ones and ends as a conservative publicist. He is the teacher, then the adversary, of Marx, and the ancestor of the mythicist thesis.

#### From the Hegelian right to the Hegelian left

Bauer makes his name in 1835-1836 with a hostile review of Strauss's *Life of Jesus*, in the name of speculative orthodoxy. His own critique of the Gospel of John (1840), then of the Synoptics, the *Critique of the Gospel History of the Synoptics* (1841-1842), tips him over: against Strauss, for whom myth is the unconscious work of the community, Bauer maintains that the Gospels are conscious literary compositions, Mark having invented the narrative that Matthew and Luke developed; the self-consciousness of the writer, not tradition, is the source. He comes to doubt that there is any historical Jesus beneath these texts, and his last books, *Christus und die Caesaren* (1877), have Christianity born in the second century, under the emperors and without a founder, from the meeting of Seneca's Stoicism and Philo's Alexandrian Judaism. In 1841 he publishes anonymously *The Trumpet of the Last Judgement against Hegel the Atheist and Antichrist*, the *Posaune*, a pamphlet in which, feigning the voice of an indignant pietist, he shows that Hegel's philosophy, properly understood, dissolves religion and the state into self-consciousness; Marx was to collaborate on its sequel. In 1842 the Prussian ministry withdraws his right to teach.

#### The Free Ones and the Jewish question

With his brother Edgar, Stirner, Köppen and others, Bauer forms in Berlin the circle of the Free Ones, die Freien, where critique becomes an end in itself: pure critique, standing above the mass and its illusions. In *The Jewish Question* (1843), he argues that Jews cannot be emancipated as Jews and that political emancipation requires the renunciation of all religion; Marx replies in *On the Jewish Question* that political emancipation leaves religion in place within civil society and that human emancipation is needed. *The Holy Family* (1845) and *The German Ideology*, by Marx and Engels, take as their target Saint Bruno and his critical criticism. After 1848, Bauer draws closer to the conservatives and writes antisemitic works.

#### Legacy

Engels, in 1882, pays tribute to him for having raised the question of the origins of Christianity as a historian, while judging that he went too far; Albert Kalthoff, Arthur Drews and the mythicists claim him as their forerunner; Albert Schweitzer devotes an admiring chapter to him; Marx, in 1844, starts from the observation that the critique of religion is complete, which he owes to Bauer's circle.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with the difference between Strauss and Bauer over the author of the myth, Bauer's doubt about the existence of Jesus, Engels's text of 1882, the circle of the Free Ones in which Stirner writes *The Ego and Its Own*, and Marx's polemic against Saint Bruno. It places Bauer at the origin of the mythicist path, which Drews, Couchoud and, according to the essay, the late Onfray take up.
