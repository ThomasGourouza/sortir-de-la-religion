Georges Bataille (1897-1962), converted to Catholicism at seventeen and out of the faith around 1920, archivist-palaeographer, librarian at the Bibliothèque nationale and then in Carpentras and Orléans, the driving force behind journals, *Documents*, *Acéphale*, *Critique*, and a novelist under pseudonyms, built, away from the university, a thought of excess, transgression and the sacred without God that marked Foucault, Derrida and the whole of French thought after 1960.

#### Documents, expenditure, Acéphale

In *Documents* (1929-1930), Bataille sets against Breton's surrealism a base materialism, which looks at what idealism sets aside, the big toe, the formless, waste. *The Notion of Expenditure* (1933), read through Mauss's *The Gift* and the potlatch, argues that societies are governed not by utility and accumulation but by unproductive expenditure, luxury, mourning, war, cult, play, sacrifice, and that the bourgeoisie, ashamed to spend, has broken the link between wealth and glory. Contre-Attaque (1935) attempts a riposte to fascism on its own ground; *Acéphale* (1936-1939), a journal and a secret society, seeks to found a religion without God or leader, Nietzschean, centred on sacrifice, and the College of Sociology (1937-1939), with Caillois and Leiris, applies Durkheim and Mauss to a sacred sociology of the present, of power and of festival, in the hope of reactivating the sacred against disenchanted society.

#### Inner experience and the Summa

During the war, Bataille writes the *Atheological Summa*, *Inner Experience* (1943), *Guilty* (1944), *On Nietzsche* (1945): a mysticism without object, a method of ecstasy through anguish and non-knowledge, which borrows from the Christian mystics and from yoga and refuses all salvation; Sartre sees in it a new mystic who keeps the corpse of God. The novels, *Story of the Eye* (1928), *Madame Edwarda* (1941), *Blue of Noon* (1935, published in 1957), in which Simone Weil appears in the guise of Lazare, stage the same experience through eroticism and abjection.

#### General economy and the sacred

*The Accursed Share* (1949) extends expenditure to a general economy: the energy received from the sun always exceeds what a living system can use to grow, and the surplus must be spent, gloriously or catastrophically; Aztec sacrifice, Islam, Lamaism, the Reformation and the Marshall Plan are its successive solutions. Sovereignty is the moment when man consumes without regard for the future, outside the servitude of the project. *Theory of Religion* (posthumous, 1973) describes sacrifice as the restoration to the intimacy of the sacred world of what labour has reduced to the state of a thing, and *Erotism* (1957) makes the transgression of the prohibition, which confirms it, the heart of sacred experience as of erotic experience: eroticism says yes to life even in death. *Critique*, founded in 1946, remains his journal.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with expenditure, the College of Sociology, Acéphale, *Inner Experience*, Sartre's attested response and the portrait of Weil, and states that it has read no extract of *Theory of Religion*. It holds Bataille to be a crucial witness, not an exception, since he refounds a sacred of transgression and confirms Durkheim, and notes that Onfray takes up the word atheology in another sense.
