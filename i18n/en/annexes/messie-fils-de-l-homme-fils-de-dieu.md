The first Christians said who Jesus was through titles borrowed from the Jewish Scriptures and from the Greek world. Their history shows how a crucified Galilean prophet received, within a few decades, the names that would lead to the definitions of the councils.

#### Messiah and Christ

Messiah, in Hebrew, and Christ, in Greek, mean anointed. The word first designates the king, and sometimes the priest, consecrated by anointing; after the end of the monarchy, it names the descendant of David awaited to restore Israel, a figure that remains varied in the time of Jesus, a king, a priest, sometimes both at Qumran. The gospels show Jesus reticent about the title, and the inscription on the cross, king of the Jews, indicates that Rome executed him as a messianic claimant. In Paul, Christ has become almost a proper name, Jesus Christ, which presupposes an already long-standing usage.

#### Son of Man

The expression, which can simply mean a man in Aramaic, is the one Jesus uses most often to speak of himself, and others almost never. It also refers to the vision of Daniel, in which one like a son of man receives everlasting kingship. The gospels use it for his present activity, for the announced suffering and for the coming in glory. Historians have debated for a century which of these uses go back to Jesus.

#### Son of God

In the Hebrew Bible, son of God is said of the angels, of the people of Israel and of the king, adopted on the day of his enthronement. The title first expresses a relation of election and obedience. The gospels have it spoken by the voice at the baptism, by the demons and, in Mark, by the centurion before the cross. John gives it a new meaning, that of the only Son come from the Father, and Paul quotes an ancient formula in which Jesus is established Son of God by the resurrection. The ontological sense, the same nature as the Father, is fixed in the fourth century at Nicaea.

#### Lord, Logos, Lamb

Lord, Kyrios, is the word by which the Septuagint renders the divine name; applying it to Jesus, as the Aramaic prayer Marana tha, which Paul preserves, already does, amounts to giving him a place beside God in worship, which Larry Hurtado regards as the most remarkable fact of nascent Christianity. The Logos of the prologue of John, the creative Word, intersects with Jewish Wisdom and with the philosophy of Philo. The Lamb of God, in John and in the Apocalypse, unites the paschal sacrifice and the suffering servant of Isaiah.

#### From title to dogma

The titles do not form a system in the New Testament; they accumulate. The quarrels of the following centuries, over the divinity of the Son and then over the union of his two natures, consist in drawing a coherent doctrine from them, from Nicaea in 325 to Chalcedon in 451. Historians thus distinguish what Jesus may have said of himself, what the first communities confessed, and what the councils defined.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay recalls, in the section on the existence of Jesus, that Reimarus saw in Jesus a political Jewish messiah who failed and that Albert Schweitzer made him a prophet of the end; in Strauss, the community awaiting a Messiah gives Jesus the features that the Scriptures attributed to the Messiah, birth in Bethlehem and descent from David; in Philo, the Logos becomes flesh; in Nietzsche, the Idiot and the only Christian, who died on the cross, stand against the titles of glory. It assumes familiarity with the passage from the crucified preacher to the Christ of the creeds.
