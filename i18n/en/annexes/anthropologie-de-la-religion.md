The anthropology of religion studies the beliefs and rites of human societies, first those that Europe called primitive, then all of them. It supplied the critique of religion with its theories of origin and sociology with its materials, and several authors of Part Nine of the essay belong to it.

#### Victorian evolutionism

The discipline was born in Darwin's England. Edward Tylor, in *Primitive Culture* (1871), defines religion at its minimum as belief in spiritual beings and derives all its forms from animism, the philosophy of the first humans, who explained dreams and death by a separable soul; religions evolve from animism to polytheism and then to monotheism, and leave survivals in modern societies. James Frazer, in *The Golden Bough* (1890-1915), arranges an immense compilation according to a sequence of magic, religion, science, and studies sacred kings put to death, gods who die and rise again, scapegoats. Robertson Smith, as early as 1889, puts sacrifice and the communion meal at the centre, which Durkheim and Freud would take up. This armchair anthropology works from the accounts of missionaries and travellers, and judges the beliefs it classifies.

#### Fieldwork and function

The twentieth century breaks with evolutionism through fieldwork. Bronisław Malinowski, stranded in the Trobriand Islands during the First World War, founds participant observation and describes magic, religion and science as three domains coexisting within one society: magic steps in where technique no longer suffices, religion answers death, myth is the charter of institutions. Radcliffe-Brown and British functionalism, Evans-Pritchard among the Azande and the Nuer, show the internal coherence of belief systems and refuse to treat them as errors. Durkheim, who did no fieldwork but read the ethnographers of Australia, draws from totemism a general theory of religion as a social fact. In France, Mauss, Lévy-Bruhl, who attributed a prelogical mentality to primitive peoples before retracting it, then Lévi-Strauss, who analyses myths as structures of thought, form another lineage.

#### Symbols, violence, ontologies

After 1960, the interpretive anthropology of Clifford Geertz defines religion as a system of symbols that establishes dispositions and conceptions of a general order of existence; Victor Turner studies ritual and liminality, Mary Douglas purity and pollution. René Girard, a literary critic who came to anthropology, proposes in 1972 a theory of the origin of the sacred in the lynching of a scapegoat victim, which professional anthropologists have received with reservation. More recently, Philippe Descola and the anthropology of ontologies take up Tylor's animism and make of it not an error but one of four ways of distributing interiority and physicality among beings. Talal Asad has shown that the very notion of religion, as an inner belief separate from the political, is a construction of the modern West.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay places Tylor and Frazer in the regime of error in its evolutionist form, retains from Malinowski that belief recedes with uncertainty and not with knowledge, reads Durkheim through totemism, and keeps the anthropological layer of Girard while leaving aside the apologetics. It takes as known the break between armchair anthropology and field anthropology, and the refutation of evolutionism by Durkheim and Wittgenstein.
