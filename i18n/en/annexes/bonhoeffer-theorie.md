Dietrich Bonhoeffer (1906-1945), son of a Berlin psychiatrist, doctor of theology at twenty-one, pastor in Barcelona and London, a student in New York, a lecturer in Berlin until he was banned, director of the Confessing Church's clandestine seminary at Finkenwalde, a member of the conspiracy against Hitler, arrested in April 1943 and hanged at Flossenbürg camp on 9 April 1945, left an interrupted body of work whose prison letters have become a classic of twentieth-century theology.

#### The Church and discipleship

The two dissertations, *Sanctorum Communio* and *Act and Being*, defended in 1927 and 1930, conceive the Church as Christ existing as community and confront revelation with philosophy. After 1933, Bonhoeffer is among those who, from the very first day, reject the Aryan paragraph in the Church and join the Confessing Church. *Nachfolge* (1937), *Discipleship*, translated into French as *Le Prix de la grâce*, opposes cheap grace, forgiveness without repentance, baptism without discipline, which has ruined German Protestantism, to costly grace, the grace that calls one to follow Christ, and comments on the Sermon on the Mount as an order to be carried out. *Life Together* (1939) describes the rule of Finkenwalde. Having returned from America in 1939 to share the fate of his people, Bonhoeffer enters the Abwehr resistance through his brother-in-law Hans von Dohnanyi and takes part in preparing the assassination attempt.

#### The Ethics

The *Ethics*, written between 1940 and 1943 and published in 1949 by his friend Eberhard Bethge, rejects an ethics of abstract principles: the question is not how to be good, but how the reality of God, revealed in Christ, becomes real among human beings; one must act responsibly, accept guilt, and the mandates, work, marriage, government, Church, structure common life. Tyrannicide is conceived as a guilty responsibility.

#### The prison letters

*Widerstand und Ergebung*, *Letters and Papers from Prison*, published by Bethge in 1951, collects the letters written from Tegel prison. From April 1944, Bonhoeffer sketches in them a new theology: the world has come of age and does without God as a working hypothesis; religion, understood as metaphysics and inwardness, which made God a stopgap at the limits of knowledge and distress, is coming to an end; Christianity must become non-religious, interpret the Bible in a worldly sense, and speak of a God who lets himself be pushed out of the world onto the cross, powerless and weak, and who helps by his weakness. He writes that before God and with God we live without God. Barth put a positivism of revelation in the place of religion, Bultmann remained a liberal; what is needed is an arcane discipline and a life for others. These fragments have nourished death of God theology, the political theology of Moltmann and Metz, and contradictory readings, secular and evangelical.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay draws only on the prison letters of 30 April, 16 and 18 July 1944, and takes as known Bonhoeffer's resistance and death, while stating that it has not verified the phrase religionless Christianity. It sees in them the reversal of Feuerbach, Marx and Nietzsche, a weak God being no longer a projection but a dispossession, and concludes that there is an exit, on condition of not leaving Christ: Bonhoeffer remains within revelation.
