Twentieth-century Christian theology responded to the critique of religion in two ways: by separating faith from religion, Barth, Bonhoeffer, or by translating faith into the terms of modernity, Bultmann, Tillich, Rahner. The essay reads its protagonists in its tenth part and draws up, at the end of its section on Marion, an assessment: a victory and a capitulation.

#### Protestantism: dialectical, existential, political

The liberal theology of the nineteenth century, Ritschl, Harnack, Troeltsch, is shattered by the war of 1914 and by Barth's *Römerbrief* (1919, recast in 1922): dialectical theology, Barth, Gogarten, Brunner, Bultmann, reaffirms the transcendence of God and the judgement of revelation on every religion. The group splits in the 1930s. Barth builds the *Church Dogmatics*; Bultmann, an exegete, proposes in 1941 to demythologize the New Testament, that is, to interpret its mythical language, the three-storeyed world, miracles, the end of time, as the expression of an understanding of existence, using Heidegger's concepts, so that the kerygma may reach modern man; Jaspers objects that myth cannot be translated without loss. Paul Tillich, who emigrated to the United States, develops a theology of correlation, in which revelation answers the questions of culture, and defines God as what concerns us ultimately, the ground of being beyond the God of theism; Bonhoeffer, in his prison letters, sketches a non-religious Christianity. After 1960, death of God theology, Altizer, Hamilton, Van Buren, radicalizes Bonhoeffer and Nietzsche; Moltmann's theology of hope (1964), nourished by Bloch, and Metz's political theology turn eschatology back towards history; process theology, inspired by Whitehead, conceives of a God in becoming.

#### Catholicism: from modernism to Vatican II

The condemnation of modernism in 1907 closes Catholicism to critical exegesis for half a century; Neo-Thomism, Maritain, Gilson, is the official philosophy. The nouvelle théologie of the 1940s, Henri de Lubac, Daniélou, Chenu, Congar, returns to the Fathers and to Scripture, rethinks the relation of nature and the supernatural and prepares the council, after being sanctioned by *Humani generis* in 1950. Karl Rahner, a German Jesuit, builds a transcendental theology of man as hearer of the Word, open to the absolute, and coins the notion of anonymous Christians; Hans Urs von Balthasar sets against him a theological aesthetics; Joseph Ratzinger, an expert at the council, then prefect of the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith and Pope Benedict XVI, defends the rationality of faith against relativism in *Introduction to Christianity* (1968) and in his dialogue with Habermas (2004). Vatican II (1962-1965) consecrates these currents and opens the post-conciliar crisis, between *Concilium*, a progressive journal, and *Communio*, founded by Balthasar, de Lubac and Ratzinger.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with dialectical theology, demythologization and the attested debate with Jaspers, the positivism of revelation for which Bonhoeffer reproaches Barth, death of God theology, the nouvelle théologie and the dialogue between Habermas and Ratzinger, which it has not read. It draws from this century the conclusion that purified faith has given up knowing, consoling, founding and instituting, and that what remains of it is a spirituality that atheists also claim.
