Paul-Henri Thiry, Baron d'Holbach (1723-1789), a German from the Palatinate naturalized French, wealthy, a chemist and mineralogist, contributor of several hundred articles to the *Encyclopédie*, kept in Paris for thirty years the salon where Diderot, Grimm, Helvétius, Hume and foreign visitors gathered, the Holbachian coterie. Under borrowed names and false imprints, he published the first systematic atheist work of modern Europe.

#### The Système de la nature

The *Système de la nature ou Des lois du monde physique et du monde moral* (1770), which appeared under the name of an academician who had died ten years earlier, sets out in two volumes a complete materialism. Nothing exists but matter and motion, which is inherent in it; nature is a whole from which everything derives by necessary laws; man is a physical being, his soul a function of the body, his freedom an illusion, and his morality must be founded on enlightened self-interest and the common happiness. The second part attacks religion: ignorance of natural causes and fear engendered the gods, priests exploited that fear, and theology, by lending God contradictory attributes, describes an impossible being; Voltaire's deism is as fragile as Christianity. The atheist, who knows the laws of nature, is more virtuous than the devout, and a society has no need of God. The book is condemned to be burned by the Parlement, refuted by Voltaire and by Frederick II, and causes a scandal even among the philosophes, Diderot excepted, who may have collaborated on it.

#### The other writings

*Le Christianisme dévoilé* (1766), *La Contagion sacrée* (1768), the *Histoire critique de Jésus-Christ* (1770), *Le Bon Sens* (1772), a popular summary of the *Système*, and a *Théologie portative* attack revelation, miracles, the Bible, the clergy and the alliance of throne and altar; *La Politique naturelle* and the *Système social* (1773) develop a utilitarian and reformist politics, enlightened monarchy rather than revolution. D'Holbach takes from Lucretius the explanation of religion by fear, from Spinoza and Bayle certain arguments, and gives the whole a pedagogical and militant form that will make its fortune in the nineteenth century, among freethinkers and in the labour movement, and in the twenty-first among the new atheists.

#### The religion of Nature

The *Système de la nature* nonetheless ends with a prayer: d'Holbach addresses Nature, sovereign of all beings, and her adorable daughters, Virtue, Reason, Truth, asking them to be for ever our only divinities. Atheist materialism thus gives itself a cult, on the model of what it fights, and it is this reversal that Voltaire points out, and that the essay sets, by construction, beside Stirner's formula.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with the salon, the *Système de la nature*, the thesis that ignorance engenders the gods, the figure of the virtuous atheist and the final prayer to Nature. It places d'Holbach in the regime of error, sees in him a projection before Feuerbach, and classes him among the transfers of the sacred: a pious atheist before Stirner, who made Nature a divinity. Voltaire answers him in the article Dieu, dieux, an attested response.
