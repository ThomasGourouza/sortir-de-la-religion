Auguste Comte (1798-1857), a student of the École polytechnique expelled in 1816, secretary to Saint-Simon, from whom he parted in 1824, examiner and then tutor at the École polytechnique, removed from the first post in 1844 and from the second in 1851, then lives on subscriptions from his disciples. He invented the word sociology, the word altruism, positivism itself, and founded a religion of which he was the high priest.

#### The Cours and the law of three stages

The *Cours de philosophie positive*, sixty lectures published from 1830 to 1842, starts from the law of three stages: each branch of knowledge passes through a theological stage, fetishist, polytheist and then monotheist, in which things are explained by supernatural wills; a metaphysical stage, in which they are explained by abstract entities; and a positive stage, in which causes are given up in favour of seeking the laws of phenomena through observation and reasoning. The sciences have followed one another in this order according to their increasing complexity, mathematics, astronomy, physics, chemistry, biology, and the last, social physics or sociology, which Comte founds in the final volumes, must in its turn become positive. Sociology comprises a statics, order, and a dynamics, progress. Positivism rejects metaphysics as it rejects theology, but it is not a materialism: it ranks the sciences, defends the unity of knowledge and subordinates science to its social end, to foresee in order to be able to act.

#### The subjective turn and the religion of Humanity

In 1844 Comte meets Clotilde de Vaux, who dies in 1846; from this year of unrequited passion dates the second career, in which feeling takes precedence over intelligence. The *System of Positive Polity* (1851-1854) and the *Catechism of Positive Religion* (1852), conversations between a woman and a priest of Humanity, found the positive religion: its object is the Great Being, Humanity, made up of the dead, the living and those yet to be born; its motto, Love as principle, Order as basis, Progress as end; its worship, private, domestic and public, includes sacraments, nine of them from presentation to incorporation, a calendar of thirteen months dedicated to great men, festivals and temples; its clergy, positivist philosophers with Comte as high priest; its morality, altruism, living for others. Religion is defined as what regulates the individual and rallies human beings together. Littré and the scientific positivists then part from Comte, while the religious disciples, Laffitte and later the Brazilian positivists, who inscribe Ordem e Progresso on their flag, carry on the cult.

#### Posterity

Positivism shaped Taine, Renan, Durkheim, who keeps its sociology and rejects its religion, the Third Republic and its schoolteachers, John Stuart Mill, who admired and then fought it, and logical positivism, which kept only its name. Löwith sees in it a history of salvation in three ages; Hayek, a source of scientism.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes the reader knows the law of three stages, the classification of the sciences, the subjective turn, the content of the *Catechism* and the formula about destroying only what one replaces. It takes Comte to be the avowed strict refoundation, the proof by fact of Stirner's law, the believing version of the Grand Inquisitor, the example of an exit from religion that refounds one with all its parts, and it reports Durkheim's attested criticism of artificial cults.
