Ernest Renan (1823-1892), a Breton from Tréguier and a seminarian at Saint-Sulpice, leaves the Church in 1845 for philology, becomes one of the first orientalists of France, is suspended from the Collège de France in 1862 for having called Jesus an incomparable man, then dismissed in 1864, returns in 1870 and becomes its administrator. Historian of religions, essayist, moralist, he embodied for two generations faith in science and elegant scepticism.

#### The break and science

The *Recollections of My Youth* (1883) recount the crisis of 1845: it is the historical criticism of the Bible, learned together with Hebrew, and not philosophy, that takes away his faith; he leaves the seminary keeping the morality and the sensibility of the priest. *The Future of Science*, written in 1848 and published in 1890, professes that science, and first of all philology, the science of the products of the human mind, will replace religion and organize humanity; the preface of 1890 admits to illusions. Renan remains to the end an idealist for whom the universe tends towards God, who is not but becomes, and a smiling sceptic in the *Philosophical Dialogues* and the *Philosophical Dramas*.

#### The Life of Jesus

The first volume of the *History of the Origins of Christianity*, the *Life of Jesus* (1863) is written after a journey to Galilee, whose landscapes seem to him a fifth gospel. It applies the German methods, Strauss, Baur, while softening them, and makes Jesus a man, a young Galilean prophet of exquisite gentleness, preacher of a religion of the heart, who lets himself be drawn little by little towards messianism, wonder-working and death, and whose cult, says the last page, will be endlessly renewed. The book, written like a historical novel, sells hundreds of thousands of copies, is translated everywhere, condemned from the pulpit, and provokes a war of pamphlets. The six following volumes, *The Apostles*, *Saint Paul*, *The Antichrist*, *The Gospels*, *The Christian Church*, *Marcus Aurelius*, follow Christianity to the end of the 2nd century, then the *History of the People of Israel* goes back to the sources.

#### The nation, races, the moralist

The lecture *What Is a Nation?* (1882) defines the nation by consent, a daily plebiscite, against the criteria of race and language of the Germany victorious in 1870. Renan nevertheless wrote, in his works on the Semitic languages, pages on the inferiority of the Semitic mind that postcolonial criticism, Edward Said, has pointed out, and on the superiority of the European races. The *Prayer on the Acropolis*, in the *Recollections*, expresses his religion of Greek beauty. He was one of the masters of the Third Republic, and his statue in Tréguier was unveiled in 1903 despite Catholic protests.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay takes for granted the crisis of 1845, the positivist method of the *Life of Jesus*, the fifth gospel, the last page on the cult that will be renewed, and Renan's place in the history of the quests for the historical Jesus. It sees in him an exit that preserves religion as a cult of the great man, reports the attested responses of Nietzsche, for whom Renan remained a priest in his entrails, and of Dostoevsky, and notes his contemporary heirs.
