The essay uses, without defining them, the terms of Christian theology that its authors criticize, defend or turn around. This vocabulary became fixed between Augustine and the Reformation, and each word in it carries a debate. The main ones are presented here, in their most common sense among theologians.

#### Revelation, faith, reason

Revelation is the act by which God makes himself known to human beings, through the history of Israel, through Christ and through the Scripture that bears witness to it; it is opposed to what reason can reach by itself. Hence the distinction, fixed by scholasticism, between natural theology, the knowledge of God through reason starting from the world, and revealed theology, which receives its principles from Scripture and tradition. Faith is assent to revelation; Augustine and Anselm make it precede understanding, Thomas Aquinas distinguishes it from knowledge while holding the two to be compatible, Luther defines it as trust, Barth rejects all natural theology. A dogma is a truth of faith defined by the Church, whose denial is heresy; a mystery, a revealed truth that reason can neither demonstrate nor fully understand, such as the Trinity or the Incarnation. The supernatural designates what exceeds the powers and the demands of created nature, grace and the vision of God; the dispute over its relation to nature, revived by Henri de Lubac in 1946, runs through Catholic theology in the twentieth century.

#### Sin, grace, salvation

Original sin is the doctrine, formulated by Augustine against Pelagius at the beginning of the fifth century on the basis of Paul (Rm 5, 12), according to which Adam's fault corrupted human nature and is passed on to all his descendants, who cannot save themselves by their own powers. Grace is the free gift by which God saves; its relation to human freedom divides Augustine and Pelagius, Luther and Erasmus, the Jansenists and the Jesuits, Pascal writing the *Provincial Letters* in this last dispute. Predestination is the doctrine according to which God has chosen from all eternity those who will be saved; Augustine teaches it, Calvin pushes it as far as double predestination, to salvation and to damnation, and Weber makes it the psychological mainspring of Puritan asceticism. Salvation is deliverance from sin and death, through the redemption accomplished in the death and resurrection of Christ; justification, at the centre of the Reformation, is the act by which God declares or makes the sinner just, by faith alone according to Luther, by faith quickened by charity and works according to the Council of Trent. Eschatology deals with the last things, judgement, resurrection, the kingdom of God, and apocalyptic with their imminence.

#### God, Christ, Church

The Trinity, fixed at the councils of the fourth century, confesses one God in three persons; the Incarnation, the Word made flesh, and Chalcedon in 451 defines Christ as one person in two natures. Negative or apophatic theology says what God is not; affirmative, or cataphatic, theology proceeds by analogy. The Church is the people of the baptized and the institution that governs it; the sacraments, seven for Catholics, two for Protestants, are the efficacious signs of grace. Theodicy, a word of Leibniz's, is the justification of God in the face of evil; apologetics, the rational defence of the faith; kenosis, the self-abasement of God in the Incarnation, on which Bonhoeffer and Weil meditate under the name of the weakness of God.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes this vocabulary is known when it opposes the God of revelation to the God of reason, when it reads Barth's critique of religion as a theology of revelation alone, when it reads in Pascal the hidden God and the wager, or when it distinguishes, in Bonhoeffer and Weil, a faith without religion. It itself recalls that religion in the strong sense involves dogmas, a community and an external absolute, features that this vocabulary helps to recognize.
