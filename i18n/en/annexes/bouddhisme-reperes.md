Buddhism was born in northern India in the 5th century BCE from the teaching of Siddhārtha Gautama, the Buddha, the Awakened One, and today counts some five hundred million followers in Asia and beyond. A religion without a creator God, but not without gods, nor without rites or institutions, it served the European philosophers of the nineteenth century as a mirror and a projection, and the essay makes it the test of any definition of religion by God.

#### The teaching

According to tradition, Prince Gautama, born at Lumbinī c. 560 BCE according to the traditional chronology, c. 480 according to the revised chronology, leaves the palace on discovering old age, sickness and death, practises asceticism, abandons it, and attains awakening under the tree at Bodhgayā; he preaches for forty-five years and dies at Kuśinagar. His first sermon, at Sārnāth, sets out the Four Noble Truths: everything is suffering, duḥkha, unsatisfactoriness; suffering has an origin, craving, desire, hatred and ignorance; it has a cessation, nirvāṇa, the extinction of craving; there is a path to this cessation, the Noble Eightfold Path, right view, thought, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness and concentration. The teaching rests on dependent origination, every phenomenon arising from conditions, on impermanence, and on non-self, anātman: there is no substantial soul, only aggregates, and what transmigrates according to karma, the law of deeds, is not a person but a stream. The Buddha refuses to answer metaphysical questions, the eternity of the world, existence after death, as useless for liberation; the gods exist, but are subject to the cycle like human beings, and no one saves anyone.

#### The schools and history

The community, the saṅgha, monks and nuns supported by lay people, forms with the Buddha and the Dharma, the doctrine, the triple refuge that every Buddhist pronounces. After the master's death, councils fix the canon and the schools divide: the Theravāda, school of the Elders, preserved in Pali in Ceylon, Burma, Thailand, Cambodia and Laos, where the ideal is the arhat; the Mahāyāna, the Great Vehicle, born around the beginning of the Common Era, which exalts the bodhisattva who renounces nirvāṇa to save all beings, develops Nāgārjuna's philosophy of emptiness and the mind-only school, and spreads to China, Korea and Japan, in the forms of Chan and Zen, Pure Land and Tiantai; the tantric Vajrayāna, in Tibet and Mongolia, with its lamas and its Dalai Lama. The emperor Aśoka, in the 3rd century BCE, makes it a state religion and a missionary religion; Buddhism almost disappears from India after the 12th century. Monastic institutions, cults of relics and images, funeral rites, ties with the powers that be, wars and persecutions, including recent ones, make it a complete religion.

#### The Buddhism of the Europeans

Known in fragments before the nineteenth century, Buddhism is constituted as an object by philology, Burnouf in 1844, and read by Schopenhauer, Renan and Nietzsche as a nihilism, a cult of nothingness, a European image that Droit has studied, or, in Nietzsche, as a positivist religion without God; Droit and Lopez have shown what this image owed to Europe. Twentieth-century Western Buddhism, Suzuki's Zen, vipassanā meditation, Kabat-Zinn's medicalized mindfulness, is a selective reinvention of it.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay takes as known the Four Noble Truths, nirvāṇa, karma, the saṅgha and the triple refuge, the division into vehicles, and the European construction studied by Droit. It draws from them that religion is not defined by God, that a doctrine without sacrifice can have sacrificial institutions, and that Buddhism is, for its thesis, neither an exception nor an ally but a test; corporate meditation seems to it a form of palliative care.
