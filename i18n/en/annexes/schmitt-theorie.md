Carl Schmitt (1888-1985), a Catholic jurist from Westphalia, professor of public law at Bonn, Berlin and Cologne, an adviser in the last years of Weimar, who joined the Nazi Party in May 1933 and was the regime's crown jurist until his fall from favour in 1936, barred from the university after 1945, living in retirement in Plettenberg until his death, is one of the most controversial political thinkers of the twentieth century, read on the right as on the left for his critique of liberalism.

#### Decision and the exception

*Political Theology* (1922) opens with the sentence: sovereign is he who decides on the state of exception. Against the normativism of Kelsen, for whom law is a system of norms closed upon itself, Schmitt maintains that the legal order rests on a decision that derives from no norm, and that the situation of exception, in which the norm is suspended, reveals the true sovereign: this is decisionism. The third chapter states that all the significant concepts of the modern theory of the state are secularized theological concepts, by their history and by their structure, the sovereign corresponding to God and the exception to the miracle; deism, which banishes the miracle from the world, corresponds to the constitutional state, which banishes the exception. Schmitt claims the counter-revolutionaries as his own, de Maistre, Bonald and above all Donoso Cortés, who had understood, in the aftermath of 1848, that the hour of decision against anarchy had come, dictatorship against discussion.

#### The political

*The Concept of the Political*, *Der Begriff des Politischen* (1927, revised in 1932), defines the political by the specific criterion of the distinction between friend and enemy: the political is not one domain among others but the degree of intensity of an association or dissociation of human beings, whose extreme case is war; the enemy is public, hostis, not private, inimicus, and a world without the possibility of war would be a world without politics. Liberalism, which seeks to dissolve the political into economics and discussion, is mistaken about human nature, and parliamentary democracy rests on a fiction. *Political Romanticism* (1919) and *The Crisis of Parliamentary Democracy* (1923) prepare these theses, *Dictatorship* (1921) and *Constitutional Theory* (1928) develop them. Leo Strauss, in his review of 1932, objects that Schmitt remains a prisoner of the liberalism he combats.

#### 1933 and after the war

In 1934 Schmitt justifies the murders of the Night of the Long Knives in an article entitled The Führer Protects the Law, organizes a conference on Jewry in legal science, and is pushed aside by the SS in 1936. Interned in 1945, he refuses denazification. *The Nomos of the Earth* (1950) traces the spatial order of European international law and its dissolution; the *Theory of the Partisan* (1963) and *Political Theology II* (1970), a reply to Erik Peterson, who held political theology to be impossible in Trinitarian Christianity, and to Blumenberg, bring his work to a close. His influence extends to Kojève, Koselleck, Böckenförde, Agamben, Mouffe and the New Right.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with the sentence on the sovereign, the theorem of secularized theological concepts, the analogy between the exception and the miracle, which it has not verified textually, Donoso Cortés, the commitment of 1933 and the correspondence with Blumenberg. With Hebekus, it reads Schmitt as a truth to be embraced rather than a denunciation, and ranks him among the strict refoundations, the limit case of refoundation in the sole form of authority.
