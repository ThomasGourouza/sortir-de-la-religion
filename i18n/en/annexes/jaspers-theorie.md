Karl Jaspers (1883-1969), a psychiatrist in Heidelberg who moved to psychology and then to philosophy, professor at Heidelberg, dismissed in 1937 because his wife was Jewish and he refused to separate from her, reinstated in 1945, emigrated to Basel in 1948, friend and then adversary of Heidegger, teacher of Hannah Arendt, founded with Heidegger the German philosophy of existence and proposed a philosophical faith between revealed religion and unbelief.

#### From psychiatry to existence

*General Psychopathology* (1913), constantly revised, introduces into psychiatry the method of understanding of Dilthey and Weber, distinguishes understanding from explaining, and remains a classic. *Psychology of Worldviews* (1919) describes attitudes and pictures of the world and introduces limit situations, death, suffering, struggle, guilt, in which man runs up against what he cannot change and in which existence awakens. *Philosophy* (1932), in three volumes, *Philosophical World Orientation*, *Illumination of Existence*, *Metaphysics*, sets out the system: objective knowledge does not exhaust being; existence, Existenz, is what man is in freedom, in communication with others, in historicity and in limit situations; transcendence is given only in failure, Scheitern, and through ciphers, Chiffern, scripts that one deciphers without ever possessing them. The Encompassing, das Umgreifende, developed in *Reason and Existenz* (1935) and *Von der Wahrheit* (1947), names the modes of the being that encompasses us, world, consciousness, spirit, existence, transcendence.

#### Philosophical faith and history

*The Question of German Guilt*, *Die Schuldfrage* (1946), distinguishes criminal, political, moral and metaphysical guilt and calls on the Germans to examine themselves. *Philosophical Faith* (1948) defines a faith without revelation, without dogma and without Church, which holds transcendence to be real without knowing it, and *Philosophical Faith in the Face of Revelation* (1962) confronts it with Christianity: revelation freezes the cipher into an objective reality, and Jaspers, in his debate with Bultmann on demythologization (1954), defends myth against its translation. *The Origin and Goal of History*, *Vom Ursprung und Ziel der Geschichte* (1949), proposes the Axial Age, Achsenzeit, between 800 and 200 BCE, in which China, India, Iran, Israel and Greece independently discovered man and transcendence, the axis of a universal history that would not be Christian. *The Great Philosophers* (1957) and political essays, on the atomic bomb, on the Federal Republic, complete the work.

#### Debates

Camus sees in Jaspers's leap a philosophical suicide and the absurd become god; Barth reproaches him with a transcendence without content; Heidegger, whose correspondence with Jaspers has been published, shares according to the essay the structure of expectation, without the communication that saves Jaspers; Arendt, Ricoeur and Jeanne Hersch made him known in France and in the United States.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay takes as known limit situations, the Encompassing, ciphers, failure, philosophical faith, the attested debate with Bultmann, the Axial Age and Camus's attested critique. It ranks Jaspers among the cautious believing exceptions, judges Camus's critique sound in logic but not with regard to the criterion, since the leap leads to no obedience, and expresses a reservation about the inflation of ciphers.
