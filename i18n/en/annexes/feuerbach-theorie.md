Ludwig Feuerbach (1804-1872), son of an eminent Bavarian jurist and a student of Hegel in Berlin, was denied a university career after his first book. He lived in retirement at Bruckberg, in Franconia, on the income of his wife's porcelain factory, and there wrote the books that made him, for the whole Hegelian left, the liberator. He died poor, a member of the Social Democratic Party.

#### From death to religion

The *Thoughts on Death and Immortality* (1830), published anonymously, deny personal immortality: only the human species is eternal, the individual dies entirely, and belief in the hereafter turns people away from life. Once the author was identified, the book closed the university to him. There followed a history of modern philosophy, a Bayle, a Leibniz, and a critique of Hegel (1839) charging the system with being nothing but a disguised theology.

#### The Essence of Christianity

*Das Wesen des Christentums* (1841) applies to religion a method he calls genetic or critical: showing what the religious object is made of. Religion is man's relation to his own essence, taken for another being: God is the human essence, reason, will, love, infinite in the species, projected outside man and worshipped; the consciousness of God is man's self-consciousness, and theology is anthropology. The first part reads each dogma as a human truth: the Trinity is the life of the species, the Incarnation the love of God for man, that is, of man for man, the miracle the fulfilment of a wish, prayer the dialogue of the heart with itself. The second part denounces the contradictions of theology, which separates God from man and sacrifices morality to faith, essentially intolerant. The remedy is not to abolish religion but to turn it around: man must take back what he gave to God, and the love of man for man must become religion.

#### The philosophy of the future and nature

The *Provisional Theses for the Reform of Philosophy* and the *Principles of the Philosophy of the Future* (1843) extend the critique to speculative philosophy, whose secret is theology: subject and predicate must be reversed, the starting point must be sensuous man, the body, the I and the Thou, whose unity is God, and man must be made the supreme being for man. *The Essence of Religion* (1846) and the Heidelberg Lectures, given to students in 1848-1849 during the revolution and published in 1851, shift the emphasis: before being the projection of the human essence, religion is a feeling of dependence on nature, and desire is its origin. In 1845 Feuerbach replies to Stirner, who accuses him of having deified Man, and turns the epithet of pious atheist back against Stirner. Marx, who owes him his break with Hegel, criticizes him in the *Theses* of 1845 for an abstract man and a contemplation without practice; Engels, in 1886, for a cult of love.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay takes as known the method of projection, the Gattung, the formulas of 1841 and 1843, the reply to Stirner and the naturalist turn of 1846 and 1851. It devotes its pivotal section to Feuerbach, sees in him the textbook case of soft refoundation and of the need to consecrate, and follows his readers: Stirner, Marx, Engels, Barth, who grants him that he is right all along the line, and Freud and Durkheim, whom the essay, by construction, reads as his transpositions.
