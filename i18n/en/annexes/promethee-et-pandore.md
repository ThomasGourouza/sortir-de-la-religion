Prometheus is the Titan who deceives Zeus for the benefit of men, gives them fire and pays for this gift with eternal torment. His myth tells at once of the origin of sacrifice, of technology and of human misfortune, and from Antiquity to Romanticism it became the figure of the man who challenges the gods.

#### Hesiod: Mecone and Pandora

In the *Theogony*, during a division at Mecone between gods and men, Prometheus carves up an ox and presents two portions to Zeus: on one side the meat and the entrails hidden under the hide and the stomach, on the other the white bones wrapped in glistening fat. Zeus chooses the appetizing portion and uncovers the bones; since then, men burn the bones for the gods and eat the meat. The myth thus founds the division of the sacrifice and the separation of gods and men. To take revenge, Zeus hides fire; Prometheus steals it and brings it back to mortals in a fennel stalk. Zeus then has the first woman fashioned, Pandora, adorned with all the gifts of the gods, whom Epimetheus, the improvident brother, accepts despite the warnings; she lifts the lid of the jar, and all evils spread over the earth, only hope remaining inside. Prometheus, chained to the Caucasus, sees an eagle devour his liver each day, which grows back at night, until Heracles delivers him.

#### Aeschylus and Plato

The *Prometheus Bound* attributed to Aeschylus makes the Titan the benefactor of humanity, to whom he gave fire, but also numbers, writing, medicine and the art of foresight, and the rebel who refuses to bend before a tyrannical Zeus whose secret he knows. In the *Protagoras*, Plato has him give men the arts, but not the political art, which Zeus alone grants.

#### The Prometheus of the moderns

From the Renaissance onwards, Prometheus becomes the symbol of man the creator. Goethe, in a poem of 1774, has him defy Zeus and declare that he shapes men in his own image, who will have no need of gods. Shelley writes a *Prometheus Unbound*, Mary Shelley subtitles *Frankenstein* the Modern Prometheus, and Marx, in the preface to his thesis of 1841, makes Prometheus the noblest saint and martyr in the philosophical calendar. Camus, in *The Rebel*, makes him one of the first figures of metaphysical rebellion, and Blumenberg devotes the last part of *Arbeit am Mythos* to the reworkings of the myth, from Goethe's poem to its exhaustion.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with Goethe's poem, Mary Shelley's *Frankenstein* and Kafka's legends of Prometheus, which the box in the Blumenberg section relates to the self-assertion of the modern age, as well as with the idea, present in the poem, of gods who live off the sacrifices of men, the essay's criterion read in reverse.
