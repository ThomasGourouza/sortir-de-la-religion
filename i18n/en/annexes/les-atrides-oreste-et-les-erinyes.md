The house of the Atreids, descendants of Tantalus and Atreus, is in Greek legend the family in which crime calls for crime over several generations, until a court puts an end to it. Aeschylus drew from it the *Oresteia*, the only surviving tragic trilogy, which recounts the passage from vengeance to the justice of the city.

#### The curse

Atreus, king of Mycenae, to take revenge on his brother Thyestes, who had seduced his wife, had him served at a banquet the flesh of his own children. From this horror a curse is born. The son of Atreus, Agamemnon, leader of the Greeks before Troy, sacrifices his daughter Iphigenia at Aulis to obtain favourable winds. During the ten years of the war, his wife Clytemnestra takes as her lover Aegisthus, the surviving son of Thyestes. On Agamemnon's return, she kills him in his bath, together with the Trojan captive Cassandra, and reigns with Aegisthus.

#### The Oresteia

Aeschylus's trilogy, performed in 458 BCE, comprises three plays. In *Agamemnon*, the king returns victorious and dies murdered. In *The Libation Bearers*, his son Orestes, an exile, returns at the command of Apollo, finds his sister Electra at their father's tomb, and kills Aegisthus and then his mother. At once the Erinyes, archaic divinities of blood vengeance, rise up to pursue him. In *The Eumenides*, Orestes takes refuge at Delphi and then in Athens; Athena institutes a court of citizens to judge him, the Areopagus. The votes are equal, and the goddess tips the balance towards acquittal. The Erinyes, appeased, accept a cult in the city and become the Eumenides, the Kindly Ones. The cycle of private vengeance is closed by an institution.

#### The other versions

Sophocles and Euripides each wrote an *Electra*, centred on the sister who awaits her brother's return, and Euripides an *Orestes* in which the hero, pursued by madness, is condemned by the assembly of Argos. In the twentieth century, Giraudoux wrote an *Électre*, Eugene O'Neill transposed the trilogy to the America of the Civil War, and Sartre, in 1943, staged *The Flies*, in which Orestes refuses the remorse that Jupiter and Aegisthus impose on the city of Argos.

#### The readings

The *Oresteia* has been read as the story of the birth of law, public justice replacing blood vengeance; as that of the passage from a matriarchal order to a patriarchal order, according to Bachofen and then Engels; and, in René Girard, as the description of a reciprocal violence that stops only through institution, sacrificial or judicial.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with Orestes, Clytemnestra and the Erinyes, whom the box in the Sartre section encounters in *The Flies*: Orestes there leaves God through an act and carries off the flies like a scapegoat, thus keeping from religion the form of sacrifice.
