Karl Marx (1818-1883), born in Trier to a Jewish lawyer converted to Protestantism, doctor of philosophy with a dissertation on Democritus and Epicurus, journalist on the *Rheinische Zeitung*, exiled to Paris, Brussels and then, from 1849, London, where he lives on Engels's subsidies and works in the British Museum, founded with Engels the socialism that bears his name and dominated the political thought of the following century.

#### The critique of religion and of Hegel

The early texts start from Feuerbach and the Hegelian left. The *Introduction to the Critique of Hegel's Philosophy of Right* (1844) regards the critique of religion as completed in Germany and as the presupposition of all critique: religion is the consciousness of an inverted world, the expression of real distress and the protest against it, the sigh of the oppressed creature, the opium of the people; one must therefore criticize the world that needs illusions, and the critique of heaven turns into the critique of earth. The *Manuscripts of 1844* analyse the alienation of labour: the worker is dispossessed of his product, of his activity, of his species-being and of other human beings, and communism is the reappropriation of the human essence. The *Theses on Feuerbach* (1845) break with contemplation: the human essence is the ensemble of social relations, and the philosophers have only interpreted the world, the point is to change it. *The German Ideology* (1845-1846), unpublished until 1932, founds historical materialism: the production of material life determines the forms of consciousness, and ideology, religion, morality, philosophy, is the inverted reflection of real relations, in which the ruling ideas are those of the ruling class.

#### History and Capital

The *Communist Manifesto* (1848), written with Engels, tells history as the history of class struggles, hails the revolutionary work of the bourgeoisie, which has dissolved religious fervour in commercial calculation, and announces the victory of the proletariat. *Capital*, of which only the first volume appears in Marx's lifetime (1867), analyses the commodity, value, the surplus value extorted from wage labour, accumulation and the laws of development of capitalism, which produce their own negation. The chapter on the fetishism of commodities shows relations between people taking the form of relations between things, and compares this inversion with the religious world, of which Christianity, the cult of abstract man, is the form suited to commodity society. Communist society, in which the associated producers regulate their exchanges in full consciousness, will no longer need this religious reflection.

#### Readings

Marxism has been read as a science of history by Engels and the Second International, as a humanism by the readers of the *Manuscripts*, and as an epistemological break by Althusser; Löwith saw in it a secularized eschatology, Ricoeur one of the three masters of suspicion, Aron and Voegelin a secular religion, Michel Henry a philosophy of life. Marx wrote little on religion after 1846; it is Engels who developed the historical study of Christianity.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with the complete text of the opium passage, including the Protestation, the *Theses on Feuerbach*, fetishism and the cult of abstract man, the transparent society, and the readings of Löwith and Blumenberg. It judges that Marx dismissed the sacred without dismissing salvation, but that he remains right against the sages through the Protestation, and in its conclusion seeks to hold Spinoza and Marx together.
