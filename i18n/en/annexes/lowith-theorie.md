Karl Löwith (1897-1973), a native of Munich of Jewish origin, wounded in 1915 on the Italian front, a pupil of Husserl and of Heidegger, whose close friend he was before becoming his most lucid critic, driven out of Marburg in 1934, exiled to Rome, to Japan, where he taught at Sendai, and then to the United States, back in Heidelberg in 1952, is the historian of philosophy who showed what the modern thought of history owed to theology.

#### From Hegel to Nietzsche

*Nietzsche's Philosophy of the Eternal Recurrence of the Same* (1935) reads the doctrine of recurrence as the attempt to recover, after the death of God, an ancient, cosmic and cyclical vision of the world, and is one of the first serious engagements with Nietzsche against the Nazi annexations. *Von Hegel zu Nietzsche* (1941), *From Hegel to Nietzsche*, retraces the revolutionary break in nineteenth-century thought: after Hegel's system, which reconciled reason and Christianity, mediation comes apart among the Young Hegelians, Feuerbach, Ruge, Marx, Stirner, Bauer, Kierkegaard, and ends in Nietzsche's radical critique of the bourgeoisie, of culture and of Christianity. The book, written in Japan, remains the great history of the Hegelian left and brings to light the series of authors whom the essay rereads as accusing one another of having remained religious.

#### Meaning in History

*Meaning in History* (1949), translated into German as *Weltgeschichte und Heilsgeschehen*, world history and salvation history, and into French as *Histoire et salut*, goes back in time from Burckhardt to the Bible: Marx, Hegel, Proudhon, Comte, Condorcet, Turgot, Voltaire, Vico, Bossuet, Joachim of Fiore, Augustine, Orosius. The thesis is that the modern philosophy of history, which attributes to history a meaning, a direction and an end, was born not of reason but of biblical eschatology, whose structure, the expectation of a final fulfilment, it has preserved, replacing providence by progress: it is a secularized eschatology. The Greeks, who thought in terms of the cosmos and the cycle, knew nothing of this question; Christianity, which hopes for a beyond of history, has no philosophy of history; the moderns, in seeking to realize Christian hope in the world, produced a hybrid form, unstable and dangerous, of which Marxism, Jewish messianism and prophetism transposed, and Comte's positivism, which ends in the founding of a religion, are the examples. Löwith concludes in favour of a withdrawal: neither faith nor progress, but a sceptical and Stoic wisdom before the cosmos, which Habermas called the Stoic withdrawal from historical consciousness.

#### Heidegger and the legacy

Löwith wrote *Heidegger, Thinker in a Destitute Time* (1953), the first book to link the commitment of 1933 to the philosophy of existence, and recounted in *My Life in Germany Before and After 1933* his meeting in Rome with Heidegger wearing the party badge. Blumenberg made *Meaning in History* the target of *The Legitimacy of the Modern Age*; Voegelin and Taubes are close to him; his thesis remains the reference point of every discussion of secularization.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay takes as known *From Hegel to Nietzsche*, *Meaning in History*, the reading of Marx and of Comte, the Stoic withdrawal and the debate with Blumenberg, an attested response. It holds Löwith to be the best historical support for its thesis and an ambiguous ally, who refounds nothing but proposes no way of living, and specifies that its criterion owes more to Stirner than to Löwith.
