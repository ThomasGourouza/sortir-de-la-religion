Peter Berger (1929-2017), a Viennese émigré to the United States and a Lutheran, and Thomas Luckmann (1927-2016), Slovene by origin, professor in Frankfurt and then in Konstanz, both students of Alfred Schütz at the New School in New York, wrote together in 1966 a book that has become a classic and then, each on his own, two books on religion that shaped the sociology of the 1960s and 1970s.

#### The social construction of reality

*The Social Construction of Reality* (1966) applies Schütz's phenomenology of the lived world to the sociology of knowledge: what we take to be self-evident reality is produced socially, through a three-stage process, externalization, human beings produce a world through their activity, objectivation, this world faces them as a thing, internalization, they take it in through socialization. Institutions arise from typified habit and are legitimated by narratives and theories, and symbolic universes, religion among them, crown legitimation by integrating the whole of experience into an order of meaning. Man produces society and society produces man. The book, written in plain language, founded social constructionism, and its authors later distanced themselves from the relativist uses that were made of it.

#### Berger: the sacred canopy and after

*The Sacred Canopy* (1967), translated into French as *La Religion dans la conscience moderne*, applies the scheme to religion: every society builds a nomos, an order of meaning that protects against chaos, and religion is the sacred canopy that relates it to a cosmos; it holds through plausibility structures, and theodicy makes suffering bearable by giving it a meaning. The second part analyses secularization as an effect of pluralism and of Christianity itself, whose transcendent God disenchanted the world, and predicts the privatization of religion. Berger later qualified this, *A Rumor of Angels* (1969) looking for signals of transcendence in ordinary experience and *The Heretical Imperative* (1979) describing modernity as the obligation to choose, and in 1999 retracted the secularization thesis: the world is as furiously religious as ever, Western Europe and intellectuals being the exception. He also wrote on capitalism, development and humour.

#### Luckmann: the invisible religion

*The Invisible Religion* (1967), the English version of a German book of 1963, defines religion by its anthropological function, the transcendence of biological nature through a universe of meaning, and argues that this function, once performed by the Churches, is now performed by private systems of meaning, assembled from themes available on a market, self-realization, family, sexuality, mobility; secularization is therefore not the end of religion but its migration out of institutions. Luckmann also contributed to the sociology of language and communication.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with social construction, the nomos, the canopy, plausibility structures, the retraction of 1999, the remark that a choice does not have the authority of a world, and the invisible religion. It draws two terms from them, the shelter of meaning, which does not disappear, and the institution, which can recede, and judges that Luckmann's definition, by encompassing everything, no longer distinguishes what the sacrifice criterion seeks to distinguish.
