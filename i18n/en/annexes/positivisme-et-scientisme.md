Positivism is the doctrine of Auguste Comte, which limits knowledge to the laws of phenomena and makes science the model of all knowledge; scientism, a word coined by its opponents at the end of the nineteenth century, designates the belief that science can answer every question, including moral and metaphysical ones, and replace religion. The essay meets both in Comte, Renan, Strauss, the freethinkers and, by inheritance, the new atheists.

#### From Comte's positivism to the positivists

In the *Cours* Comte had set out positive philosophy, then founded in the *Système* a religion of Humanity; his disciples split. Émile Littré, the lexicographer, and the scientific positivists keep the method, the law of the three stages and the classification of the sciences, reject the religion and the priesthood, and give the word positivist its ordinary meaning: a supporter of a philosophy of science, hostile to metaphysics and theology, republican and secular. Pierre Laffitte and the religious positivists perpetuate the cult, in France and in Brazil. Stuart Mill, in England, and the logical positivism of the Vienna Circle, in the twentieth century, keep the name while changing the thing. Positivism, in its various forms, trains the elites of the Third Republic, Gambetta, Ferry, and inspires secular schooling, medicine, the methodical history of Langlois and Seignobos, and the sociology of Durkheim, who both claims it and departs from it.

#### Scientism

Alongside strict positivism, the nineteenth century saw the flowering of a faith in science that goes beyond method. Renan, in *The Future of Science*, written in 1848, expects science to organize humanity and replace religion; Taine applies to history and literature the laws of race, milieu and moment, and declares that vice and virtue are products like vitriol and sugar; Berthelot, the chemist turned minister, proclaims in 1885 that the world is henceforth without mystery, and in 1897 dedicates a morality to science; Haeckel, in Germany, draws from Darwin a monism that he sets up as a religion, and founds a Monist League in 1906; Büchner, Vogt and Moleschott spread a popularized materialism. In France, free thought, organized in societies from the 1860s onwards, combines anticlericalism, a cult of reason and of science, and civil rites, secular funerals and baptisms. Brunetière, in 1895, announces the bankruptcy of science, and the word scientism, which spreads at the turn of the century, serves the spiritualists, and later Hayek and Popper, to denounce the illegitimate extension of scientific method.

#### Posterity and critiques

Nietzsche sees in faith in scientific truth the last form of the ascetic ideal; Weber sets science, which gives no meaning, against the prophets of the lectern; Husserl speaks of a crisis of the European sciences; the new atheists of the twenty-first century, Dawkins and Harris in particular, have been accused of scientism by their critics, and Science written with a capital letter is, for the essay, one of the essences in Stirner's sense, a comparison it presents as constructed.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay takes as known the difference between Comte's positivism and Littré's, the scientism of Renan and Berthelot, the monism of Haeckel, which touches Drews, and free thought. It ranks the Reason and the Science of the new atheists among the Stirnerian essences, turns § 344 of *The Gay Science* against Freud's faith in science, which holds against any scientism, and reports that Durkheim proves Comte right on substance and wrong on method.
