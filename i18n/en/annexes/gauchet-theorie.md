Marcel Gauchet (b. 1946), historian and philosopher, a student of Claude Lefort, co-founder with Pierre Nora of the journal *Le Débat* in 1980, and director of studies at the École des hautes études en sciences sociales, proposed in *The Disenchantment of the World* a political history of religion that makes Christianity the religion of the exit from religion, and devoted his subsequent books to the democracy that this exit makes possible and fragile.

#### The Disenchantment of the World

The 1985 book takes up Weber's word while shifting it: disenchantment is not only the retreat of magic, it is the exhaustion of the reign of the invisible as the organizing principle of societies. Religion in its pure form, that of the stateless societies studied by Pierre Clastres, is heteronomy: the law comes from an untouchable founding past, people do not think of themselves as the authors of their order, and this dispossession protects them from division and from power. The appearance of the State, then of the great religions with a transcendent God, in Jaspers's axial age, breaks into this structure: the further God withdraws, the more the world is left to human beings. Christianity, through the incarnation of a God who enters history and through its absolute transcendence, carries this movement to its end: it makes thinkable an autonomous world, a State that detaches itself from the Church, an individual who relates directly to God, and thus produces the conditions of its own effacement. Democratic modernity is the fulfilment of this dynamic, not its negation. The exit from religion is completed around 1700, according to Gauchet, and we live in its aftermath.

#### Democracy and the religious after religion

*La Révolution des droits de l'homme* (1989), *La Révolution des pouvoirs* (1995) and *L'Avènement de la démocratie*, in four volumes from 2007 to 2017, trace the constitution of modern autonomy, the State, law, history, and its crises, from totalitarianism to the democracy of individuals. *La Religion dans la démocratie* (1998) specifies that the exit from religion does not mean the exit from religious belief: religion persists as identity and as private choice, and French laïcité changes meaning when the Church ceases to be a rival of the State. *La Condition historique* (2003), a book of interviews, sums up the trajectory; *Le Religieux après la religion* (2004), a dialogue with Luc Ferry that the essay says it has not read, sets two readings of what remains against each other. Gauchet has also written on schooling, on psychiatry, with Gladys Swain, on the unconscious and on the crisis of transmission.

#### Debates

Historians have discussed the dating, sociologists the absence of fieldwork, theologians a reading of Christianity that makes it the instrument of its own end; Pierre Manent, Charles Taylor and Hans Joas have engaged in dialogue with him, and the 1985 book remains the reference work of the French debate on secularization.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay takes as known heteronomy, the religion of the exit from religion, the role of the incarnation, the 1998 distinction between the exit from religion and the exit from belief, and the dialogue with Dumont. It brings Gauchet together with the third essay of Nietzsche's *Genealogy*, § 27, with § 357 of *The Gay Science* and with Girard, in constructed confrontations, uses him against Onfray's *Décadence*, and holds him to be the historian of the exit, not the thinker of what remains.
