Paul Ricoeur (1913-2005), a war orphan brought up in Reformed Protestantism, *agrégé* in philosophy, a prisoner in Germany from 1940 to 1945, where he translated Husserl, professor at Strasbourg, at the Sorbonne, at Nanterre, of which he was dean in 1969, and at Chicago, built a body of work in which phenomenology passes through hermeneutics and through dialogue with the human sciences, psychoanalysis, linguistics, history, exegesis.

#### The will and the symbol

The *Philosophy of the Will* begins with a phenomenology of the voluntary and the involuntary (1950), then, in *Finitude and Guilt* (1960), approaches evil through man's fallibility and through *The Symbolism of Evil*: fault is expressed first neither in concepts nor in pure experiences, but in symbols, defilement, sin, guilt, and in myths, among them the Adamic myth; the symbol gives rise to thought, and philosophy must pass through the interpretation of symbols in order to recover, beyond criticism, a second naivety. This detour grounds the hermeneutic graft on phenomenology.

#### Suspicion and conflict

*Freud and Philosophy: An Essay on Interpretation* (1965) reads psychoanalysis as a hermeneutics of suspicion, which reduces the apparent meaning to the economy of the drives, and contrasts it with the hermeneutics of the restoration of meaning, that of Eliade's phenomenology of religion, which listens to the symbol; Marx, Nietzsche and Freud form the school of suspicion, masters who taught us to doubt consciousness, and Ricoeur holds that faith must pass through this critique instead of refusing it. *The Conflict of Interpretations* (1969), which includes Religion, Atheism, and Faith, adds that the idols must be allowed to die, the god of accusation and the god of consolation, in order to hear the symbols, and maintains that the rival hermeneutics, structuralism, psychoanalysis, phenomenology of religion, cannot be reduced to one another and that reflective philosophy must arbitrate between them without abolishing them. Ricoeur kept his philosophy separate from his faith, set out in articles of exegesis and biblical theology, while letting them answer each other.

#### The text, the narrative, the self

*The Rule of Metaphor* (1975) shows that metaphor creates meaning and redescribes reality; the theory of the text distinguishes the world of the text, opened up in front of it, from the intention of the author. *Time and Narrative* (1983-1985) maintains that human time becomes thinkable only when narrated, through emplotment, in history as in fiction, and that identity is narrative. *Oneself as Another* (1990) develops a hermeneutics of the self, between the exaltation of the cogito and its Nietzschean humiliation, and a little ethics: aiming at the good life, with and for others, in just institutions. *Memory, History, Forgetting* (2000) and *The Course of Recognition* (2004) complete the journey.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay takes for granted the school of suspicion, the distinction between the two hermeneutics, the second naivety, the symbol that gives rise to thought and the text Religion, Atheism, and Faith on the two gods rightly destroyed. It holds Ricoeur right on method and wrong about the object, the three regimes of suspicion remaining distinct, reports the objection that the second naivety might be merely a first naivety giving itself a clear conscience, and classes Ricoeur among the believing exceptions, prudent and unstable.
