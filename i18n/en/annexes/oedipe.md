Oedipus is the Theban hero who, without knowing it, kills his father and marries his mother. His legend, already known to Homer, received its classical form in the tragedies of Sophocles, and in the twentieth century it became one of the most commented narratives of European culture, by psychoanalysis, structural anthropology and mimetic theory.

#### The legend

An oracle of Apollo foretold to Laius, king of Thebes, that his son would kill him. The child, his feet pierced, hence his name of swollen foot, is exposed on Mount Cithaeron, taken in by shepherds and raised in Corinth as the king's son. Grown up and warned in his turn by the oracle that he will kill his father and marry his mother, he flees Corinth, kills at a crossroads an old man who was blocking his way, Laius, then delivers Thebes from the Sphinx by solving its riddle: what being walks on four legs in the morning, on two at noon, on three in the evening. He becomes king and marries the widowed queen, Jocasta, with whom he has four children, Eteocles, Polynices, Antigone and Ismene.

#### Oedipus Rex

Sophocles' tragedy begins years later: plague ravages Thebes, and the oracle demands that the murderer of Laius be driven out. Oedipus leads the inquiry relentlessly; the blind seer Tiresias accuses him, he suspects Creon of a plot, and the successive witnesses gradually reveal the truth. Jocasta hangs herself, Oedipus puts out his eyes and asks for exile. In *Oedipus at Colonus*, Sophocles' last play, the old wandering Oedipus, guided by Antigone, dies mysteriously near Athens and becomes a protecting hero for the city that welcomes him.

#### Modern readings

Freud sees in the legend the expression of a universal desire of the child for the parent of the other sex and of its rivalry with the other parent, the Oedipus complex, which in *Totem and Taboo* he extends to the origin of religion through the murder of the primal father. Claude Lévi-Strauss analyses the myth as a system of oppositions, between overrated kinship and underrated kinship, autochthonous origin and sexual origin. Jean-Pierre Vernant insists on the reversal: the saviour king becomes the defilement to be expelled, like the *pharmakos*, the scapegoat victim that certain cities drove out during festivals of purification.

#### Girard's reading

René Girard reads *Oedipus Rex* as the narrative of a sacrificial crisis: the plague figures the contagion of violence in which differences fade, the symmetrical accusations between Oedipus, Tiresias and Creon show doubles, and the expulsion of Oedipus restores order. Parricide and incest would be the typical crimes that a crowd attributes to its victim; the tragedy would half show the mechanism, the myth would hide it entirely.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with the oracle, the plague of Thebes and Oedipus's inquiry, which the Girard section summarizes, with Freud's Oedipus complex and the murder of the father in *Totem and Taboo*, as well as with Becker's rereading, which, after Otto Rank, makes the Oedipus complex a causa sui project, the child's desire to become his own father.
