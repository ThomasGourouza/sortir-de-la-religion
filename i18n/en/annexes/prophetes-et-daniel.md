The prophets occupy a whole section of the Hebrew Bible, and their books are among those most often cited by the New Testament. Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel and the twelve minor prophets, to whom the Christian Bible adds Daniel, shaped the ideas of covenant, judgement and messiah.

#### What a prophet is

In the Bible, the prophet is not first of all a soothsayer but a spokesman: he speaks in the name of God, often against the king, the priests and the people. A distinction is drawn between court prophets, such as Nathan before David, and the so-called writing prophets. Elijah and Elisha, in the ninth century, wrote nothing; the books of Kings recount Elijah's challenge to the prophets of Baal and his being taken up in a chariot of fire.

#### From the eighth century to the exile

Amos denounces the luxury of Samaria and the oppression of the poor: worship without justice displeases God. Hosea depicts the covenant as a marriage betrayed by the wife. Isaiah of Jerusalem preaches trust in God alone and announces to King Ahaz the birth of a child named Emmanuel (Is 7). Jeremiah preaches submission to Babylon, sees Jerusalem fall in 587 and announces a new covenant written on hearts. Ezekiel, deported as early as 597, prophesies in Babylon through visions. The exile (587 to 538) is the crucible of the Bible: the deportees gather their traditions together. Cyrus authorizes the return in 538; the Second Temple is dedicated around 515.

#### The three Isaiahs and the suffering servant

Since Bernhard Duhm (1892), criticism has distinguished three blocks in the book of Isaiah: chapters 1 to 39, for the most part from the prophet of the eighth century; chapters 40 to 55, an anonymous Second Isaiah who speaks to the exiles and hails Cyrus as God's anointed; chapters 56 to 66, a Third Isaiah after the return. Second Isaiah contains four songs of the servant, the last of which (Is 52 and 53) describes a despised man, struck for the faults of others, whose suffering obtains the justification of many. The same block affirms that God hides himself (Is 45).

#### Messianism

The word messiah means anointed and first designates the reigning king. After the end of the monarchy, hope shifts to a descendant of David yet to come who would restore justice.

#### Daniel

The book of Daniel presents itself as the work of a Jewish sage at the court of Babylon in the sixth century. Its first part recounts court episodes: Nebuchadnezzar's dreams interpreted, the three companions in the furnace, Daniel in the lions' den. The second part contains visions: four beasts rising from the sea, then an Ancient of Days and, coming on the clouds, one like a son of man who receives eternal kingship (Dn 7); a succession of empires told with increasing precision up to the reign of Antiochus IV Epiphanes, the Seleucid king who profanes the Temple in 167; and finally the announcement of a resurrection of the dead. Porphyry, in the third century, had noticed that the precision stops at Antiochus, and concluded that the book, written under this king, told the past in the form of prediction. Modern exegesis likewise dates its final redaction to the years 167 to 164, during the Maccabean revolt.

#### Composition and Christian rereading

The prophetic books are collections of oracles supplemented over several centuries by disciples and redactors. The first Christians read them as announcements of Christ: Matthew cites the Emmanuel of Is 7 from the Septuagint, which translates the Hebrew word for young woman as virgin; the Passion is told with the features of the servant of Is 53.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay draws on Porphyry, dating Daniel to the time of Antiochus Epiphanes, as an ancestor of critical exegesis; it cites in Pascal the verse of Isaiah on the hidden God, which the *Pensées* turn into a thesis; it places, in Girard, the suffering servant, Job and the Psalms among the texts that proclaim the innocence of the victim; and it presents, with Schweitzer and Sanders, Paul's Christianity as an eschatology, an expectation of an imminent end for which Daniel and the prophets of the exile supplied the images.
