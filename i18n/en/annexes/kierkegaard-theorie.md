Søren Aabye Kierkegaard (1813-1855), the son of a melancholy and pious Copenhagen merchant, a student of theology and the author of a dissertation on irony, broke off his engagement to Regine Olsen in 1841 and devoted the fourteen years left to him to a torrential body of work, published partly under pseudonyms and partly under his own name, which attacks Hegel, established Christendom and the forgetting of what it means to exist.

#### The pseudonyms and the stages

*Either/Or* (1843), by Victor Eremita, sets the aesthetic stage, that of the seducer who lives in the instant and in the possible, against the ethical stage, that of the husband and of Judge Wilhelm, who chooses and commits himself. *Fear and Trembling* (1843), by Johannes de Silentio, meditates on the sacrifice of Abraham: the knight of faith suspends the ethical by a leap that no one can understand or justify, and gets Isaac back by virtue of the absurd; this is the religious stage, higher than the ethical and incommensurable with it. *The Concept of Anxiety* (1844), by Vigilius Haufniensis, describes anxiety as the dizziness of freedom before the possible, bound up with sin. The *Philosophical Fragments* (1844) and the *Concluding Unscientific Postscript* (1846), by Johannes Climacus, contrast Socrates, for whom the truth lies within man, with Christ, for whom it comes from outside in the instant, and posit that subjectivity is truth: faith is the infinite passion of inwardness holding fast to an objective uncertainty, and Hegel's system, which claims to complete existence in the concept, forgets that the existing individual is in becoming. *Stages on Life's Way* (1845) takes up the triad again. *The Sickness unto Death* (1849), by Anti-Climacus, analyses despair as the sickness of the self that does not will to be itself, or wills to be itself without God, and in which sin is the contrary not of virtue but of faith.

#### The discourses and the attack

In parallel, Kierkegaard published under his own name *Upbuilding Discourses* and religious works, *Works of Love* (1847), *Practice in Christianity* (1850). In 1854-1855, after the death of Bishop Mynster, who had been praised as a witness to the truth, he launched in the press, then in his own sheet, *The Moment*, a frontal attack on the Danish state Church: Christendom, where everyone is Christian by birth, has abolished Christianity by making it flourish; the New Testament demands suffering and a break with the world, and the civil-servant pastors make a comfortable living from it. He died in the midst of the polemic, refusing the sacraments from a priest.

#### Legacy

Widely translated into German at the beginning of the twentieth century, Kierkegaard nourished the dialectical theology of Barth, who borrowed from him the infinite qualitative difference, the existentialism of Jaspers, Heidegger and Sartre, and the critique of Camus, who sees in his leap a philosophical suicide. He is also, with Pascal, the source of the critique of religion in the name of faith.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay takes as known the stages, the leap, the thesis of subjectivity, the *Postscript* and *The Moment*. It sees in Kierkegaard a reversal of Stirner, established religion as a humanism in disguise, an exit from above after Pascal and before Barth, Bonhoeffer and Weil, and objects that he provides no internal criterion between God and the idol: a limit case.
