Danièle Hervieu-Léger (b. 1947), a sociologist, director of studies and then president of the École des hautes études en sciences sociales, for many years director of the *Archives de sciences sociales des religions*, is the leading French theorist of religious modernity. Trained in the Durkheimian and Weberian tradition and a reader of Berger and Luckmann, she has proposed a definition of religion that makes it a matter of memory and transmission.

#### Religion as memory

*Vers un nouveau christianisme ?* (1986) describes the charismatic communities and the emotional revival of the 1970s as responses to secularization rather than as survivals. *La Religion pour mémoire* (1993) poses the problem of definition: substantive definitions, by the supernatural, miss religions without God, and functional definitions, by meaning, take in everything, like Luckmann's invisible religion. Hervieu-Léger proposes defining religious believing by a specific mode of legitimation: a believing is religious when it draws its authority from a tradition, that invokes the continuity of a believing lineage going back to founding witnesses, and that a chain of memory, an ideological, practical and symbolic apparatus, sustains and controls. Modernity is then a society of fragmented, amnesiac memory, where the chains break, not because people stop believing but because transmission no longer takes place; in return it produces recompositions, utopias, substitute memories, among them the nation and the revolution.

#### The pilgrim and the convert

*Le Pèlerin et le Converti* (1999) describes two figures of contemporary religiosity, which replace the regular churchgoer: the pilgrim, whose believing is mobile, individual, à la carte, made up of an itinerary and of experiences, validated by felt authenticity rather than by conformity; and the convert, who chooses a lineage instead of inheriting it, and whose intense commitment may be a change of religion, a return, or the affiliation of someone without religion. The validation of believing shifts from the institution to the individual, the group and the elective community. *La Religion en miettes* (2001) applies the analysis to sects and new movements, and *Catholicisme, la fin d'un monde* (2003) shows that the Church in France has lost, along with practice, its capacity to organize the common culture: the exculturation of Catholicism.

#### Method and debates

Hervieu-Léger works through qualitative surveys, interviews and monographs, and through the history of the social sciences of religion; she discusses Gauchet, whose diagnosis of the exit from religion she shares while insisting on recompositions, and the thesis of the religious market, which she judges ill-suited to Europe. Her definition has been criticized for leaving spiritualities without lineage outside religion, a consequence she accepts.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with the definition by the believing lineage, amnesiac modernity, the pilgrim, the convert and exculturation. It infers from them that the convert touches on the sacrifice criterion, that there are substitute lineages, Nation, Revolution, Civilization, and that a spirituality without lineage escapes refoundation but is not passed on: the modern pilgrim has no lineage, he has a library.
