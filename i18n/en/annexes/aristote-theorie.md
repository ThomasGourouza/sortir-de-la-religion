Aristotle (384-322 BCE), a pupil of Plato for twenty years and then founder of the Lyceum, left lecture treatises covering logic, physics, biology, metaphysics, ethics, politics and poetics. The essay reads above all books Λ, E and A of the *Metaphysics* and the tenth book of the *Nicomachean Ethics*; here is their context.

#### The concepts of physics and metaphysics

Against Plato's separate Ideas, Aristotle holds that forms are in things: each individual substance, this horse, this man, is a compound of matter and form. To conceive change, he distinguishes act and potency: the acorn is an oak in potency, the oak is the act of the acorn; motion is the passage from potency to act. Every complete explanation draws on four causes, material, formal, efficient and final, and nature acts for the sake of an end. Logic, set out in the *Organon*, invents the syllogism and the theory of demonstration. The *Metaphysics*, a title given by the editors to the books placed after the *Physics*, seeks the science of being qua being and of first principles; being is said in several senses, the first of which is substance. Book Λ crowns the whole with theology: the eternal motion of the heavens requires an eternal, immobile substance, pure act without matter, which moves as an object of love and whose life is thought thinking itself.

#### The soul and knowledge

The treatise *On the Soul* defines the soul as the form of a natural body having life in potency, and distinguishes the nutritive, sensitive and intellective functions. The intellect that thinks all things must be separate and impassible; the famous passage on the agent intellect, which brings intelligibles into act as light brings colours, will feed the debates of Alexander of Aphrodisias, Avicenna, Averroes and Thomas on the immortality and unity of the intellect. Knowledge begins with sensation, passes through experience and is completed in the grasp of principles.

#### Ethics and politics

The *Nicomachean Ethics* asks what the highest good for man is: happiness, eudaimonia, an activity of the soul in accordance with virtue in a complete life. The moral virtues, courage, temperance, justice, liberality, are dispositions acquired by habit and consist in a mean determined by practical wisdom; the intellectual virtues are science, art, practical wisdom, intellect and wisdom. The tenth book places at the summit the contemplative life, theôria, the most continuous, the most self-sufficient and the most divine, and invites man, as far as possible, to immortalize himself, athanatizein, by living according to what is highest in him. The *Politics* makes man a political animal, examines regimes and their corruptions, and prescribes a common education. The *Poetics* defines tragedy by the purgation of the passions, catharsis.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay takes as known the doctrine of act and potency, the theory of causes, the definition of the first mover and of thought thinking itself, which it makes the God of the philosophers of the three monotheisms, and the place of contemplation at the summit of ethics, which it reads as a spirituality of exercise without worship. It also retains the passage of Λ 8 on the myths added to persuade the multitude, the ancestor of the elitist reading, and the definition of first philosophy as theological, the source of Heidegger's onto-theology.
