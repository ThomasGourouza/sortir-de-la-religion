German idealism is the name given to the philosophical movement which, from the *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781) to the death of Hegel (1831), sought to complete Kant's undertaking by grounding all knowledge in the activity of the mind, and whose great names are Fichte, Schelling and Hegel. It supplied the nineteenth-century critique of religion with both its vocabulary and its target.

#### After Kant

Kant had limited knowledge to phenomena and left the thing in itself unknowable; his successors regard this limit as an inconsistency. Reinhold seeks a single principle; Jacobi maintains that every consistent rationalism leads to Spinozism, and will later denounce Fichte's idealism as a nihilism; and the pantheism controversy (1785), born of Jacobi's revelation of Lessing's Spinozism, puts Spinoza at the centre of the debate: the Tübingen generation, Hölderlin, Schelling, Hegel, reads him with enthusiasm. Fichte, in the *Wissenschaftslehre* (1794), starts from the absolute I that posits itself and posits the not-I, and deduces from this activity consciousness, the world and morality; freedom is the first principle, and the world is only the material of duty. Accused of atheism in 1799 for having identified God with the moral order of the world, he loses his chair at Jena, in what became the atheism controversy, and goes on to develop a doctrine of absolute being and the *Addresses to the German Nation*.

#### Schelling and Hegel

Schelling, a prodigy at twenty, adds to Fichte's I a philosophy of nature, in which nature is visible spirit and spirit invisible nature, then a *System of Transcendental Idealism* (1800), which makes art the organ of philosophy, and a philosophy of identity, in which the absolute is the indifference of subject and object, which Hegel will mock as the night in which all cows are black. After the *Philosophical Investigations into the Essence of Human Freedom* (1809), which think evil and a dark ground in God, Schelling publishes almost nothing for thirty years, teaches at Erlangen and then at Munich and, in 1841, in Berlin, where Kierkegaard, Engels, Bakunin and Burckhardt hear him, a positive philosophy, a philosophy of mythology and a philosophy of revelation, which starts from existence and not from the concept, against the dead Hegel. Hegel, the last to arrive, builds the completed system in which the absolute is spirit and knows itself through history, and in which religion is the representation of what philosophy thinks in concept.

#### The end of idealism and its sequels

Hegel's death opens the crisis: the Hegelian right and left dispute the inheritance, Feuerbach turns idealism into anthropology, Marx sets it on its feet, Kierkegaard opposes existence to the system, Schopenhauer despises it, neo-Kantianism and positivism turn away from it. Idealism is nonetheless reborn among the British neo-Hegelians, Bradley, in Croce and Gentile, in Kroner, and its study was renewed in the twentieth century by Heidegger, Hyppolite, Tilliette, Henrich and research on the Jena constellation.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with the pantheism controversy and the rediscovery of Spinoza, with Schelling's philosophy of revelation, from which it quotes the question of why there is something, and with Hegel's system. It locates in the exit from idealism the birth of the Hegelian left and of the modern critique of religion, and reports that Schelling was called to Berlin to combat the dragon's seed of Hegelian pantheism.
