Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel (1770-1831), a fellow student of Hölderlin and Schelling at the Tübingen seminary, private tutor, journalist, headmaster of a grammar school, professor at Heidelberg and then at Berlin, where he reigned over German philosophy, built the last great system: a thought of totality in which the real is rational, history has a meaning and religion finds its truth in philosophy.

#### The dialectic and the Phenomenology

Hegel's method is the dialectic: every finite determination contains its negation, and the movement by which a position passes into its opposite and is then resolved into a higher unity, which both cancels and preserves it, Aufhebung, is at once the law of thought and the law of being. The *Phenomenology of Spirit* (1807) follows this movement through the history of consciousness, from sense-certainty to absolute knowledge, by way of famous figures: the dialectic of master and slave, in which the master depends on the slave who works; the unhappy consciousness, divided between itself and an unchanging beyond, a figure of medieval Christianity; the Enlightenment against faith; the beautiful soul. The *Science of Logic* sets out the categories of pure thought, from being to the concept, and the *Encyclopaedia of the Philosophical Sciences* (1817) the whole system: logic, philosophy of nature, philosophy of spirit, subjective, objective, absolute.

#### Objective Spirit and history

The *Elements of the Philosophy of Right* (1821) set out Objective Spirit, abstract right, subjective morality and ethical life, family, civil society, State, the modern State being the realization of freedom; the preface states that what is rational is actual and that the owl of Minerva takes its flight at dusk. The lectures on the philosophy of history make world history the progress of the consciousness of freedom, accomplished by the cunning of reason through the passions of great men.

#### Religion and the concept

Absolute Spirit manifests itself in art, religion and philosophy, which have the same content, the absolute, in three forms: intuition, representation, concept. Religion tells the truth in the form of images and narratives; philosophy sublates it into concept, and thus preserves Christianity by going beyond it. The young Hegel had written critical texts on the positivity of the Christian religion; *Faith and Knowledge* (1802) ends on the feeling that God himself is dead and on the speculative Good Friday; the *Lectures on the Philosophy of Religion*, delivered four times in Berlin, present Christianity as the absolute religion, in which God reveals himself as Spirit, and read the Trinity and the Incarnation in concepts. After his death, the school splits between a right, which holds philosophy to be the ally of faith, and a left, Strauss, Bauer, Feuerbach, Marx, which draws from it the critique of religion.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with the dialectic, Aufhebung, the distinction between representation and concept, *Faith and Knowledge*, the *Lectures* and the quarrel with Schleiermacher, an attested response. It holds Hegel's argument to be sound and his conclusion false, philosophy becoming the higher religion of the wise, and follows Kierkegaard's attested response and the critique of the Hegelian left, as well as Löwith's genealogy, which places Hegel within secularized eschatology.
