André Comte-Sponville (b. 1952), a graduate of the École normale supérieure, agrégé in philosophy, lecturer at the Sorbonne until 1998, member of the National Consultative Ethics Committee, is one of the most widely read French philosophers of his generation. Brought up a Catholic and having left the faith at eighteen, he defends a non-dogmatic materialism, a morality without foundation and a spirituality without God, in the tradition of Epicurus, Spinoza and Montaigne.

#### Despair and beatitude

The *Traité du désespoir et de la béatitude* (1984-1988), in two volumes, *Le Mythe d'Icare* and *Vivre*, sets out the theses: materialism, everything is matter or depends on it, and thought is only a function of the body; despair, not as sadness but as the renunciation of hope, which in Spinoza is an anxious sadness; beatitude, joy in what is, in the present, without expectation. Comte-Sponville calls himself a materialist in knowledge and a spiritualist in practice, and reads Epicurus, Lucretius, Spinoza, Montaigne, Pascal and the Eastern wisdoms as philosophies of salvation without God. *Une éducation philosophique* (1989) and *Valeur et vérité* (1994) clarify the relation between knowledge, which is not a value, and morality, which is not knowledge.

#### Morality and the virtues

*A Small Treatise on the Great Virtues* (1995), an international success, reviews eighteen virtues, from politeness, which is not one but prepares them, to love, which dispenses with them, by way of fidelity, prudence, justice, compassion, gentleness and humility, and proposes a morality without metaphysical foundation, which holds through fidelity to what civilization has handed down to us: one can be an atheist and faithful to Christian values, because they are not Christian but human. *Le Capitalisme est-il moral ?* (2004) distinguishes four orders, the technical and economic, the legal and political, the moral, and the ethical, whose confusion produces ridicule and tyranny. The *Présentations de la philosophie* (2000) and the *Dictionnaire philosophique* (2001) set out the whole for a wide public.

#### L'Esprit de l'athéisme

*L'Esprit de l'athéisme* (2006) answers three questions. Can we do without religion? Yes, but not without communion, fidelity and love: fidelity is what remains of faith once it has been lost, and the faithful atheist keeps what Christianity taught him. Does God exist? Probably not: no proof holds, and evil, human mediocrity and the all too desirable character of the hypothesis tip the balance, but atheism is a belief, not knowledge. What spirituality for atheists? A spirituality of immanence, grounded in the mystical experience of the All, the oceanic feeling of Romain Rolland, which Comte-Sponville knew one night in a forest: fullness, simplicity, eternity, acceptance, in which the Kingdom is here and now. *La Sagesse des Modernes* (1998), a dialogue with Luc Ferry, sets this Spinozist acceptance against Ferry's Kantian transcendence.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes the reader knows the materialism, the fidelity, the three questions of 2006, the oceanic feeling taken over from Rolland, the formulas about the Kingdom and about the universe that suffices, the quarrel with Ferry and Hausemer's criticism concerning the disappearance of evil. It holds that Comte-Sponville refounds no sacrificial absolute and tends towards the position of immanence of Spinoza and Hadot, but that his oceanic experience consoles without protesting: he lacks Marx's Protestation, and he does not refound without being an exception.
