Martin Heidegger (1889-1976), son of a Catholic sexton in Messkirch, a seminarian, a student of Husserl, professor at Marburg and then at Freiburg, rector of that university in 1933-1934 under the Nazi regime, barred from the university from 1945 to 1951, dominated twentieth-century continental philosophy through a single question, that of the meaning of being, and left a body of work, the complete edition, of more than a hundred volumes.

#### Being and Time

*Sein und Zeit* (1927), unfinished, starts from the observation that the question of being has been forgotten by metaphysics, which has thought only beings. To raise it again, one must analyse the being that asks the question, man, whom Heidegger calls Dasein, being-there, to avoid the words subject and consciousness. Dasein is being-in-the-world, always already thrown into a situation, busy among things and with others; its being is care. It mostly exists in the inauthenticity of the they, idle talk and curiosity; anxiety, which has no object, brings it face to face with its own being, and being-towards-death, the resolute anticipation of its ownmost possibility, restores it to authenticity. Temporality, the future first, is the meaning of the being of Dasein, and historicity follows from it. The method is a hermeneutic phenomenology, and the destruction of the history of ontology was to follow.

#### The turn

After 1930 comes the Kehre, the turn: Heidegger no longer starts from Dasein but from being itself, which gives itself and withdraws in a history whose epochs are the figures of metaphysics, from Plato to Nietzsche, the last metaphysician, in whom the will to power completes the overturning of Platonism. Metaphysics is onto-theology: it thinks beings in general and the supreme being, and forgets the ontological difference between being and beings. Modern technology, whose essence is the Gestell, the enframing that summons every thing to stand available as standing-reserve, is the accomplishment of this forgetting, and only a god can still save us, as the interview given to *Der Spiegel* in 1966 and published in 1976 will say. Thought must then prepare another beginning by listening to the poets, Hölderlin first, and to the Presocratics, and by practising Gelassenheit. The *Letter on Humanism* (1947) answers Sartre that existentialism remains metaphysical.

#### 1933 and the Black Notebooks

Heidegger joins the Nazi party in May 1933, gives a rectorial address that places the university at the service of the movement, resigns in 1934 and remains a member until 1945. The debate on the link between his philosophy and this commitment, opened as early as 1946, was revived by Farias in 1987 and by the publication, from 2014 onwards, of the *Black Notebooks*, notebooks containing antisemitic passages tied to the history of being. Heidegger never expressed public regret.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with onto-theology, the death of God as the end of metaphysics, the causa sui, Gelassenheit, the waiting for a god, the rectorship and the *Black Notebooks*, and warns that it quotes Heidegger from no printed edition. It judges that Heidegger refounds, waiting for a god within an empty absolute whose priest is the thinker, while granting him that the critique of the God of the philosophers is not the critique of God, a point Marion will turn around.
