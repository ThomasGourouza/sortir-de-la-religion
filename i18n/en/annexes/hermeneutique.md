Hermeneutics is the art, and then the theory, of the interpretation of texts and, by extension, of every human expression. Born of biblical exegesis and philology, it becomes in the nineteenth century a method of the human sciences, and in the twentieth a philosophy. Ricoeur, from whom the essay takes the name school of suspicion and the idea of second naivety, is its heir.

#### From rules of interpretation to method

Antiquity and the Middle Ages already practised a hermeneutics without the word: the allegory of Philo and Origen, the four senses of Scripture, the taʾwīl of Averroes. The word appears in the seventeenth century to designate the rules for interpreting sacred, legal and classical texts. Schleiermacher, at the beginning of the nineteenth century, makes it a general discipline: to understand is to reconstruct the meaning of a discourse from the language, grammatical interpretation, and from the individuality of the author, psychological interpretation, and the aim is to understand an author as well as, and even better than, he understood himself. He formulates the hermeneutic circle: one understands the whole only from the parts and the parts only from the whole. Wilhelm Dilthey, at the end of the century, makes understanding, Verstehen, the proper method of the human sciences, as opposed to the explanation of the natural sciences: nature is explained, the life of the mind is understood in its expressions.

#### Philosophical hermeneutics

Heidegger shifts the question: understanding is not a method but a mode of being of Dasein, which always already finds itself within a pre-understanding, and the circle is not a defect but the very structure of existence. Hans-Georg Gadamer, in *Truth and Method* (1960), draws from this a philosophical hermeneutics: all understanding is caught up in a tradition and carried by prejudices, in the sense of prior judgements, which the Enlightenment was wrong to discredit wholesale; to understand a text is to bring about a fusion of horizons between its world and ours, and the history of a work's effects is part of its meaning. Habermas objects, in a debate begun in 1967, that tradition can be ideology and that a critique going beyond it is needed; Gadamer replies that critique itself speaks from within a tradition. Emilio Betti defends against Gadamer a methodical and objective hermeneutics.

#### Ricoeur and the conflict of interpretations

Paul Ricoeur grafts hermeneutics onto phenomenology and routes it through the human sciences. *The Symbolism of Evil* (1960) starts from symbols and myths, which give rise to thought; *De l'interprétation* (1965) confronts Freud's psychoanalysis, a hermeneutics of suspicion that demystifies, with the hermeneutics of the restoration of meaning, that of Eliade and of the phenomenology of religion, and names Marx, Nietzsche and Freud the school of suspicion. *The Conflict of Interpretations* (1969) holds that rival readings do not cancel each other out. Ricoeur then develops a theory of the text, of metaphor and of narrative, and a biblical hermeneutics that distinguishes the world of the text from the author's intention. Second naivety is the name of the faith that has passed through critique without renouncing it.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with the line of Schleiermacher, Dilthey, Heidegger, Gadamer and Ricoeur, with the sense of understanding as opposed to explanation, with the idea that interpretation has no end, through which the essay has Ricoeur answer the objection it raises against him on the basis of Hegel and Strauss, and with the fact that Habermas, in *Faith and Knowledge*, proposes a translation of religious contents, a gesture that belongs to hermeneutics. It judges that Ricoeur is right about the method and wrong about the object, the three regimes of suspicion remaining distinct.
