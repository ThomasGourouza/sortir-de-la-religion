Friedrich Daniel Ernst Schleiermacher (1768-1834), son of a Reformed chaplain, educated among the Moravian Brethren, a pastor in Berlin, a friend of the Romantics, a professor at Halle and then at the University of Berlin, which he helped to found, and a translator of Plato, is regarded as the father of modern Protestant theology and one of the founders of hermeneutics. He sought to save religion by shifting it from doctrine to experience.

#### The Speeches on Religion

The *Reden*, *On Religion: Speeches to Its Cultured Despisers* (1799), were written in the circle of the Berlin Romantics, Friedrich Schlegel, Novalis, Henriette Herz, for whom the religion of the Churches was dead. Schleiermacher answers them that what they despise is not religion: its essence is neither metaphysics, which thinks, nor morality, which acts, but the intuition and feeling of the universe, the sense and taste for the infinite; each positive religion is an individual form of this intuition, and Christianity the highest, because its central intuition is mediation. Scripture is only the mausoleum of living religion, Spinoza is one of its saints, and a religion without God can be worth more than a badly conceived religion. The book marks the birth of religious Romanticism and of the regime of experience.

#### The Glaubenslehre

*Der christliche Glaube* (1821-1822, recast in 1830-1831), *The Christian Faith*, is the dogmatics of his maturity. Piety is defined there as the feeling of absolute dependence, schlechthinniges Abhängigkeitsgefühl, and God as the whence of this feeling; dogmas are the conceptual expression of the states of Christian religious consciousness, and theology a science of faith as it is lived in a community. Christ is the man in whom God-consciousness was perfectly powerful, and redemption the communication of this consciousness. The Trinity is relegated to an appendix, and miracles and prophecies lose their role as proofs. Hegel, in the preface to Hinrichs (1822), mocks the dog, which would be the best Christian if religion were dependence; Feuerbach and Barth will say that this theology of human consciousness leads to anthropology.

#### Hermeneutics, Plato, ethics

Schleiermacher founds general hermeneutics, the art of understanding any discourse from its two sides, grammatical and psychological, and sets out the circle of the whole and the parts. His translation of Plato, unfinished, remains in use in German. His *Soliloquies* and his ethics develop a philosophy of individuality in which each person expresses humanity in his own way, and his *Dialectic* a theory of knowledge. He also played a part in the Union of the Lutheran and Reformed Churches in Prussia and an educationalist.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with the *Reden*, the definition of the feeling of absolute dependence, the Romantic circle and Schleiermacher's Plato. It makes him the first name of the regime of experience and the first attempt to leave instituted religion without leaving lived religion, and formulates against him the weakness of this whole regime: experience proves that one feels, not what one feels. Hegel, Feuerbach and Otto answer him in attested responses, and Barth draws the conclusion.
