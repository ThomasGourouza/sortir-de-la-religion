The critique of religion begins in Greece as a critique of myths. From the sixth century BCE, thinkers reproach the poets for having lent the gods unworthy actions, and seek either to purify the idea of the divine or to reinterpret the narratives so as to save their meaning. These two gestures, correction and allegory, run through the whole later history of religious critique.

#### Xenophanes and Heraclitus

Xenophanes of Colophon reproaches Homer and Hesiod for having attributed to the gods everything that is shameful among men, theft, adultery, deceit. He observes that the Ethiopians depict their gods as black and snub-nosed, the Thracians as red-haired and blue-eyed, and that if oxen could paint, they would paint ox gods. Against this projection he sets a single god, the greatest, who resembles mortals neither in body nor in thought. Heraclitus of Ephesus likewise mocks rites of purification by blood and prayers addressed to statues, and makes fire and the logos the law of the world.

#### Plato against the poets

In the *Republic*, Plato excludes from the ideal city the narratives of Homer and Hesiod that show quarrelsome, lying or changeable gods, because they shape the souls of the guardians badly. Two rules must guide the poets: the god is good and is the cause only of good; he is simple and does not deceive. Plato does not for all that give up myth: he invents some himself to say what argument cannot reach.

#### The interpretations

To save Homer, readers read his narratives as allegories: as early as the sixth century, Theagenes of Rhegium sees in the battles of the gods struggles between natural elements, and the Stoics, from Zeno to Cornutus, make Zeus fire or ether, Hera air, Athena thought. Another reading, euhemerism, comes from Euhemerus of Messene, who relates around 300 BCE that he found on a distant island an inscription proving that Zeus was a king deified after his death: the gods would be illustrious men who became objects of worship. The Epicureans, finally, keep blessed and indifferent gods, but reject the whole mythology of fear.

#### Aristotle and useful myths

Aristotle, in the *Metaphysics*, distinguishes in the tradition a true core, the divinity of the first substances, and mythical additions, the human or animal form of the gods, introduced to persuade the multitude and to serve the laws. This political reading of myth will be taken up from Antiquity to the Enlightenment.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with Xenophanes, to whom it compares a formula of Montaigne, with the Platonic rejection of fables, Aristotle's thesis on the myths added to persuade the multitude, and the allegorical method that Philo of Alexandria applies to the Bible after the Stoics applied it to Homer. It does not name Euhemerus, but the explanation of the gods by the deification of men is the distant ancestor of the theories of projection it studies in d'Holbach and Feuerbach.
