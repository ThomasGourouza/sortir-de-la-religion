Arthur Schopenhauer (1788-1860), son of a Danzig merchant and of a novelist, who took his doctorate at Jena, living on private means in Frankfurt for nearly thirty years after the failure of his lectures in Berlin, where he had scheduled them at the same hours as Hegel's, knew fame only after 1851. He built a unique system, pessimistic and atheist, nourished by Kant, Plato and the Upanishads, which shaped Wagner, Nietzsche, Freud, Thomas Mann and Wittgenstein.

#### The world as representation and as will

*The World as Will and Representation* (1819, enlarged in 1844) starts from Kant: the world is my representation, subject to the forms of the subject, space, time, causality, which Schopenhauer reduces to the principle of sufficient reason, studied in his dissertation. But the thing in itself, which Kant declared unknowable, is accessible to us by a path Kant did not see: our own body, which we know from within as will. The thing in itself is the Will, a blind willing, one, without reason and without end, which objectifies itself at every level of nature, in gravity as in the animal and in man, where it becomes the conscious will to live; the world of individuals is the veil of Maya, the illusion of multiplicity. Since all willing arises from a lack and every satisfaction opens another, life swings between suffering and boredom: this is pessimism, which Schopenhauer supports with history, biology and the observation of human beings. Art, and first of all music, the direct expression of the Will, temporarily suspends willing in the contemplation of the Ideas.

#### Morality and the denial of the will

*On the Basis of Morality* (1840) rejects Kant's imperative as a disguised theology and grounds morality in compassion, Mitleid, through which the individual recognizes in the suffering of another his own essence, through the veil of individuation. Final deliverance is the denial of the will to live, which Schopenhauer describes in the fourth book in the words of the Christian mystics, Eckhart, Madame Guyon, and of the sages of India: asceticism, chastity, poverty, resignation, up to nirvana, where this very real world, with all its suns, is nothing. He holds authentic Christianity, pessimistic and ascetic, to be superior to optimistic Judaism and Protestantism, and Buddhism to be the truest religion, because it is without God.

#### The Parerga and religion

The *Parerga and Paralipomena* (1851), the collection of essays that made his fame, contain the *Aphorisms on the Wisdom of Life* and the dialogue On Religion, between Demopheles, who defends religion as the metaphysics of the people, the allegorical garment of a truth the crowd cannot receive naked, and Philalethes, who demands the unveiled truth and denounces the crimes of religions; the dialogue reaches no conclusion. Schopenhauer professes a serene atheism, without a substitute cult, and a misogyny his readers have not forgotten.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with the doctrine of the Will, pessimism, compassion, the denial of the will to live and the dialogue of Demopheles and Philalethes, a Grand Inquisitor without the tragedy. It judges that Schopenhauer left God without leaving the religion of salvation, credits him with being the first to read India and the mystics outside theology, and follows the attested break of Nietzsche, for whom pity is the practice of nihilism.
