Johann Caspar Schmidt (1806-1856), known as Max Stirner because of his high forehead, a teacher at a girls' boarding school in Berlin and a member of the circle of the Free Ones around the Bauer brothers, wrote a single book, *Der Einzige und sein Eigentum*, *The Ego and Its Own* (dated 1845, published at the end of 1844), then translated Adam Smith and Say, ran a dairy, knew debtors' prison and oblivion, before being rediscovered by the anarchists and by Nietzsche, who may have read him.

#### The book

*The Ego and Its Own* opens and closes with the line from Goethe that sets its cause on nothing. The first part, Man, tells human history as that of the individual: the Ancients, realists, who live in the world; the Moderns, idealists, who since Christianity have lived in the spirit; and the Free Ones, political, social and humane liberals, who believe they have overcome Christianity and have merely replaced God with the State, Society and Man. Every idea placed above the individual, to which he must sacrifice himself, is a spectre, a Spuk, a fixed idea, and whoever is possessed by it has a screw loose; the sacred is what is alien to the individual and what he respects. The second part, I, sets against all these spectres the Unique One: not a concept, but the one who speaks, who acknowledges no vocation, no duty, no essence, and who holds everything, others included, as his property to the extent of his power. The Ego is a creative nothing, the nothing from which he draws everything. Revolution, which changes institutions, is opposed to insurrection, which refuses to let itself be arranged; society, to the union of egoists, a revocable association of individuals who make use of it. Stirner's egoism is not greed but self-enjoyment, and his style, piling up wordplay, parodies the Bible and Hegel.

#### The receptions

Feuerbach, Szeliga and Hess replied as early as 1845, and Stirner answered them in *Stirner's Critics*. Marx and Engels devoted to him the greater part of *The German Ideology* (1845-1846), unpublished until 1932: Saint Max has merely substituted for the domination of ideas that of one idea, the Unique One, and ignores that the individual is produced by real social relations. The book disappeared until its rediscovery by Mackay in 1898; it then became a reference for individualist anarchism, a supposed precursor of Nietzsche, a case for psychoanalysis, and for Camus, in *The Rebel*, the point where metaphysical rebellion turns into nihilism.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with Goethe's line, the theory of spectres, the sentence on our atheists as pious people, the fear of Man as a modified form of the fear of God, theological insurrections, and the replies of Feuerbach, Marx and Camus. It makes Stirner the author of the law of the whole series, the law of refoundations or Stirner's law, which Stirner himself does not escape, since the Unique One installs itself in the place of God, and it ranks the Ego among the transfers of the sacred.
