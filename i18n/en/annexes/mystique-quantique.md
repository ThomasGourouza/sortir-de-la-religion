Quantum mysticism is the name given to the contemporary spiritualities that invoke quantum physics to assert that consciousness creates or influences reality, that everything is connected and that the mind can act directly on matter. It is often associated with Jung's synchronicity and with the law of attraction, and it inherits a long tradition that, since the eighteenth century, has borrowed its vocabulary from the physics of the moment.

#### The ancestors: fluid, magnetism, thought

Franz Anton Mesmer, a Viennese physician who settled in Paris in 1778, treated patients with animal magnetism, a universal fluid he claimed to capture and transmit; a royal commission on which Lavoisier and Franklin sat concluded in 1784 that the effects observed were due to imagination. Magnetism survived into the nineteenth century, with Puységur and his disciples, and then in hypnosis. In the United States Phineas Quimby, a clockmaker turned healer, taught around 1850 that illness arises from false beliefs; from him descend Mary Baker Eddy's Christian Science (1875) and the New Thought movement, which William James described in 1902 under the name of mind-cure, and whose heirs are positive thinking and personal development.

#### Synchronicity

Jung called synchronicity the coincidence of a psychic state and an external event linked not by a cause but by a meaning, and he made it a principle of acausal connection. He set it out in 1952 in a volume shared with Wolfgang Pauli, Nobel laureate in physics, who published a study of Kepler in it; the best-known example is that of the golden scarab that strikes the window while a patient is recounting a dream about a scarab. The notion has had an immense posterity in popular spiritualities. Cognitive psychology explains the impression of meaningful coincidence by the tendency to perceive patterns and intentions in chance, which the psychiatrist Klaus Conrad named apophenia in 1958, and by selective memory, which retains coincidences and forgets non-coincidences.

#### From The Tao of Physics to The Secret

In 1975 Fritjof Capra, who holds a doctorate in physics, published *The Tao of Physics*, which brings quantum mechanics close to Hindu, Buddhist and Taoist mysticism. David Bohm, a recognized physicist, proposed in 1980 an implicate order, an undivided totality of which things would be the unfoldings, and held dialogues with Krishnamurti. Deepak Chopra, a physician, launched quantum healing in 1989. The film *What the Bleep Do We Know!?* (2004) popularized the idea that the observer creates reality, and Rhonda Byrne's *The Secret* (2006), book and film, presented the law of attraction, according to which thoughts attract the events that resemble them, as a secret known to the great men of history.

#### The physicists' answer

Physicists object that the observer of quantum mechanics designates an interaction with a measuring apparatus and not a consciousness, that entanglement transmits no information and does not connect minds, and that quantum effects vanish at the scale of the brain and the body. Murray Gell-Mann spoke of quantum flapdoodle, Victor Stenger devoted a book to refuting the thesis of a quantum consciousness, and the Sokal affair, in 1996, opened a wider debate on the use of the exact sciences by the humanities. The law of attraction is also criticized for its consequences: making the sick and the poor responsible for their lot, and sometimes turning people away from medical care.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes familiarity with Jung's synchronicity, quantum mysticism, the law of attraction and their ancestor, the mind-cure described by William James. It examines them in Part Twelve together with the *Christ Letters*, as a demand that science testify for the sacred, a Providence renamed the Universe and a theodicy without God that turns complaint into a negative thought.
