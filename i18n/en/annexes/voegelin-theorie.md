Eric Voegelin (1901-1985), born in Cologne, raised in Vienna, a pupil of Kelsen, whose adversary he became, professor in Vienna, driven out by the Anschluss and an émigré to the United States, where he taught in Louisiana from 1942 to 1958, back in Munich from 1958 to 1969 to found the institute of political science there, then professor at Stanford, sought all his life to understand the totalitarianisms as religious phenomena and to refound political science on a philosophy of order.

#### The political religions

*Die politischen Religionen* (1938), ready in Vienna in April 1938, seized after the Anschluss and republished in Stockholm at the end of the same year, argues that National Socialism and communism are not only ideologies but religions, intramundane ones, which divinize a reality of this world, the people, the race, the class, the State, and take over the structure of the sacred: hierarchy, ekklesia, apocalypse, kingdom. Voegelin goes back from Akhenaten and Egypt to the medieval religions of empire, to Hobbes and the modern State, to show that the political has always been religious and that secularization has only displaced the divine towards the world. He would later regret the word religion, too vague, and speak of experiences of order and their symbolizations.

#### The New Science of Politics and gnosticism

*The New Science of Politics* (1952), lectures given in Chicago, is his most widely read book. Political science must once again become a science of order, founded on the experience of transcendence that the Greeks and Christianity articulated. Christianity, by divesting the world of the divine, created a tension that many found unbearable, and the history of the West since Joachim of Fiore, with his three ages, is that of a relapse into gnosticism: the claim to certain knowledge of the meaning of history and to salvation through human action, the immanentization of the Christian eschaton. Puritanism, the Enlightenment, progressivism, Comte's positivism, Marxism, Nazism are gnostic movements, and totalitarianism is the final form of progressive civilization. Averroes, he says, had seen something. *Science, Politics and Gnosticism* (1959) refines the notion, and Voegelin would later extend it by speaking of egophany and of the refusal of the question.

#### Order and History

*Order and History*, five volumes from 1956 to 1987, covers Israel and revelation, the world of the polis, Plato and Aristotle, the ecumenic age and the search for order, to show how societies symbolize their experience of order and transcendence, from cosmological myth to philosophical and revealed differentiation; the fourth volume breaks with the linear plan. Voegelin was in dialogue with Strauss, Arendt, Aron, Kelsen, who answered him in a posthumous book, *Secular Religion*, and Blumenberg, who holds the modern age to be a victory over gnosticism, not a relapse.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes that the political religions of 1938, the immanentization of the eschaton, gnosticism, the sentence on totalitarianism and the remark about Averroes are known. It ranks these religions among the most massive cases of the criterion, holds Kelsen and Blumenberg to be right against Voegelin's gnostic genealogy and wrong against the function that Aron describes, and distinguishes secular religion in the functional sense, which it finds indispensable, from the Voegelinian genealogy, which proves everything.
