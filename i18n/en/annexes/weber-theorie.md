Max Weber (1864-1920), a jurist and economist from Erfurt, professor at Freiburg and Heidelberg, his career interrupted by a depression from 1898 to 1903, then a private scholar, editor of the *Archiv für Sozialwissenschaft*, a participant in German political life and in the Weimar Constitution, is, with Durkheim, the founder of modern sociology. His work, unfinished and scattered, deals with Western rationality and its religious origins.

#### The method

Weber founds an interpretive sociology: the object of the social sciences is meaningful human action, which must be understood in order to be explained. Its instrument is the ideal type, a construction that accentuates certain features of reality, capitalism, bureaucracy, charisma, to serve as a yardstick for concrete cases, without claiming to describe them. The scholar must observe value neutrality, Wertfreiheit: he may study values, not prescribe them from his lectern, for no science can decide between the gods. *Economy and Society*, a posthumous work, systematizes these concepts: the types of action, instrumentally rational, value-rational, affective, traditional; the types of legitimate domination, legal, traditional, charismatic; the forms of community, of law, of the city, of religion.

#### Religion and rationalization

*The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism* (1904-1905) shows how the inner-worldly asceticism of the Puritans, fed by the anxiety about salvation that Calvinist predestination created, produced a methodical conduct of life, work as a calling, Beruf, and accumulation without enjoyment, an elective affinity with the spirit of capitalism, which then lost its religious mainspring to become a casing hard as steel. The studies of Confucianism, Hinduism, Buddhism and ancient Judaism ask why economic and scientific rationalization took place only in the West, and build a comparative sociology of religions: ethical and exemplary prophecy, religions of virtuosos and of the masses, theodicies of suffering, ascetic and mystical paths of salvation, flight from the world or mastery of the world. The guiding thread is rationalization, a long-term process that disenchants the world, Entzauberung, by eliminating magic, begun by the prophets of Israel and completed by science. Charisma, the extraordinary gift of the prophet or the leader, is the revolutionary force that breaks routine, before becoming routinized in its turn.

#### The two lectures

*Science as a Vocation*, delivered in 1917 and published in 1919, and *Politics as a Vocation* (1919) draw the consequences: the disenchanted world no longer knows prophets, science gives no meaning, ultimate values are at war, polytheism, and each must choose his god; the politician must hold together the ethic of conviction and the ethic of responsibility. Weber dies in June 1920 of pneumonia following the Spanish flu.

#### What the essay assumes

The essay assumes that the ideal type, value neutrality, the thesis of the *Protestant Ethic*, the casing hard as steel, rationalization and disenchantment, the polytheism of values and the sacrifice of the intellect are known. It makes Weber the second representative of the regime of social function, confronts him with Durkheim on the return of the gods, notes that he had read Nietzsche and that the convergence with the *Genealogy* is not fortuitous, and wonders whether he is not the last Puritan.
